What we promise vs. what we ship

Every public commitment DURA makes — Rule 7's free-forever pledge, offline-first, the marketing numbers, the terms and privacy policy, and declared standards conformance — checked against the code and content that has to deliver it. One finding is serious: the load-bearing promise behind Rule 7 is not legally in force. The rest of the house is broadly honest.

Findings

The license is GPLv3, not AGPLv3

critical 
LICENSE:1   GNU GENERAL PUBLIC LICENSE          Version 3, 29 June 2007
expected    GNU AFFERO GENERAL PUBLIC LICENSE   Version 3, 19 November 2007

The file does contain the word “Affero” three times — all inside GPLv3 §13, “Use with the GNU Affero General Public License”, the clause that lets you link GPL code with AGPL code. AGPLv3's own §13 is “Remote Network Interaction,” and it is absent. Easy to misread; entirely different effect.

That difference is the promise. GPLv3 has no network-use provision, so this claim on the open-source page describes protection you do not have:

“Nobody gets to fork it, put it behind a paywall, and pull the ladder up.”

Under GPLv3, anyone may host DURA as a paid SaaS and never publish a line of their changes. AGPLv3 §13 is precisely the clause that closes that hole — and it is the legal backbone Rule 7 rests on.

AGPLv3 is asserted in 35 places in the UI, the README badge, README line 113, and CLAUDE.md Rule 7 — and at privacy/page.tsx:224, which makes it a misstatement inside a legal document rather than loose marketing copy. package.json declares no license field at all.

Two standards are declared enforced but have no checks

governance

“DURA enforces conformance against prose + schema specs versioned under standards/.” — CLAUDE.md

The CI job is named “Standards Conformance Gate (AINDGS / LFLRS / PPLAS)” — and that naming is accurate about what it covers. LP-1.0 (Lesson Pedagogy), FM-1.0 (Feature Module), and DLS-1.0/2.0 have no checks in tests/, scripts/, or CI. They exist as prose only.

LP-1.0 states new lessons “must conform on first commit.” Across 660 lesson files, nothing verifies that — the promise is aspirational, not enforced.

The Supabase guard overstates its own reach

accuracy

“Every Supabase call in DURA goes through this module.” — guard.ts header

Four of the 44 files calling createClient() import it, and 14 of the 15 query-layer modules bypass it entirely.

Offline-first itself still holds — progress.ts, flashcards.ts and profile.ts all wrap their calls in try/catch and fall back to IndexedDB. What does not happen is the circuit breaker: with no isFeatureAvailable() check, a dead Supabase gets hammered call by call instead of tripping open and backing off. The promise survives; the mechanism the file describes does not exist at the scale it claims.

What to do

  1. Replace LICENSE with the real AGPL-3.0 text and add "license": "AGPL-3.0-only" to package.json. Roughly ten minutes of work that converts 35 standing claims from false to true. Worth a lawyer's glance since it appears in the privacy policy — and note that relicensing stays this simple only while Durwood Studios is the sole copyright holder.
  2. Pick a side on LP-1.0 and FM-1.0. Either build them into the standards gate, or soften CLAUDE.md's “enforces” to “documents” for those two. An unenforced rule that claims enforcement is worse than an honest guideline.
  3. Correct the guard.ts header to describe the coverage it actually has — or route the query layer through it and make the sentence true.

Promises that hold

  • Rule 7 is clean in product code. No payment checks, no subscription status, no tier gates, no “upgrade to unlock” anywhere in the learning experience.
  • The paywalled: true flags are not a violation. They live in src/lib/phase-m/index.ts and describe third-party ISO/IEC industry specs that cost money from their publishers — DURA content stays open. Correct usage.
  • Marketing numbers are exact — 15 phases against 15 phase directories, 118 modules against 118 module directories. No inflation.
  • Analytics default to off behind an explicit consent gate, with three dedicated PPLAS-R4 conformance tests holding it in place.
  • XP transmission is not consent-gated, and carries a comment telling future contributors not to add such a check. The learner's own record stays theirs.
  • Terms and privacy accurately describe the tip flow — voluntary, non-refundable, Stripe-processed, no card data stored by DURA.
