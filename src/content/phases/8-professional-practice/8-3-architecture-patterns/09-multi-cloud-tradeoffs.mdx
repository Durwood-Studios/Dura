## Why this matters

A service runs in two cloud providers but relies on one identity service and one deployment pipeline. Provider diversity does not automatically eliminate the failure paths that matter to users.

## Start with something familiar

Two emergency exits do not provide independent escape routes if both lead through the same locked corridor. Multiple providers can similarly share critical dependencies that defeat the intended resilience.

## The mental model

> Multi-cloud is an architecture choice with operational costs and failure assumptions. Identify the business requirement, shared dependencies, data consistency and recovery evidence before treating provider count as a resilience metric.

## The mechanism

Start with the requirement: withstand a provider-specific outage, satisfy a particular placement constraint or negotiate a commercial risk. Different goals imply different designs. A second provider may be a cold recovery target, a warm standby or an active participant; these have different state, latency, staffing and testing obligations.

The synthetic comparison has option A: one provider at 1000 currency units/month and eight operator-hours/month; option B: two providers at 1400 units/month,30 operator-hours/month and200 units/month transfer cost. At the supplied50 units/hour staffing value, A totals 1400 and B3100 per month. The difference is 1700, before migration and incident costs. These are teaching inputs, not current vendor prices or a purchase recommendation.

Both options depend on one external identity service. If it fails and no tested recovery path exists, B does not meet the stated identity-outage continuity goal merely because compute exists twice. Draw dependencies for identity, DNS, secrets, data replication, build/deploy and observability. Test the specific outage you intend to tolerate.

Data design matters: active-active writes require an explicit conflict/consistency model; a backup in another provider needs measured restore time and recovery point evidence. A second logo on an architecture diagram does not determine how much acknowledged data can be lost or how long recovery takes. Preserve units and definitions for recovery objectives and compare them with rehearsed observations.

A decision record should include a simpler alternative, such as improving recovery within one provider if that meets the actual requirement. Choose according to evidence and constraints, not a general rule that multi-cloud is always safer or always wasteful. This exercise evaluates a supplied architecture and cost model; no provider account or cross-cloud failover is executed.
