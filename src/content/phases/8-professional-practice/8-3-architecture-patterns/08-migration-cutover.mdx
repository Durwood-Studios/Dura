## Why this matters

A migration appears reversible because traffic can be pointed back to the old database. After the new system accepts writes, the old copy is stale. Routing reversal without data reconciliation can erase acknowledged work.

## Start with something familiar

Moving a shop to a new register is easy until new sales are recorded only there. Returning to the old register requires accounting for those sales, not merely changing which counter customers approach.

## The mental model

> A cutover changes both traffic ownership and data authority. Rollback must preserve acknowledged writes and respect the point after which simple reversal is no longer safe.

## The mechanism

The fictional service has source records1–100 copied to a target. At cutover, the target accepts acknowledged writes101–105 while the source remains at 100. Returning traffic to the source now exposes stale state unless those writes are reconciled. This is a data authority problem, not only a DNS problem.

Define prerequisites: dependency inventory, target functional/performance checks, backup/restore evidence, data reconciliation, an owner and a communication plan. Choose a strategy based on actual constraints: a short write pause, staged read traffic, replication or a controlled incremental transition may suit different systems. Do not assume dual writes are atomic across systems without a designed protocol.

A runbook names checkpoints, expected evidence, go/no-go criteria, decision authority and the latest safe reversal point. For this exercise, zero acknowledged-write loss is required and a15-minute write pause is permitted. The target’s five new writes can be exported and replayed with stable IDs in six minutes, followed by a four-minute reconciliation check. Those are supplied measured rehearsal times, not estimates for an arbitrary production system.

A rollback plan can pause writes, replay101–105 to the source, reconcile counts/IDs, then reopen on the source within the ten-minute rehearsed recovery path. It must also verify that replay is idempotent and no unrecorded writes occurred during the pause. If the recovery evidence does not fit the allowed window or data cannot be reconciled, the owner may need a fix-forward plan rather than pretending reversal is safe.

After cutover, observe real business operations, not just a health endpoint. Keep the old system under a declared retirement and retention plan until recovery obligations are satisfied. A migration rehearsal on a representative disposable dataset produces useful evidence; a written runbook alone is not a successful migration.
