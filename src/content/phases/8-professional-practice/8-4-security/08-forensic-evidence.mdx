## Why this matters

A log file contains a suspicious event, but it was edited during analysis and no original copy remains. A technically interesting observation becomes difficult to trust when acquisition and handling are undocumented.

## Start with something familiar

A laboratory sample needs a label, collection record and handling history. A digital artifact also needs context: identical bytes alone do not tell you who collected them, from where or under what conditions.

## The mental model

> Preserve source evidence, document acquisition and work from a controlled copy. A cryptographic digest can detect changed bytes relative to a known digest; it does not prove origin, truth or legal admissibility.

## The mechanism

An evidence record identifies the source, collection method, collector, time with timezone, relevant clock uncertainty, permissions and subsequent handling. Preserve the original according to the incident procedure and analyze a copy. Collection itself can alter a running system, so record its method and limits rather than claiming acquisition is automatically neutral.

This complete offline fixture hashes synthetic bytes and checks a working copy. It does not acquire a disk, memory image or private log.

```python
import hashlib

original = b"2026-09-13T10:00:00Z login_failed user=test\n"
known_digest = hashlib.sha256(original).hexdigest()
working_copy = bytes(original)
assert hashlib.sha256(working_copy).hexdigest() == known_digest
changed = working_copy.replace(b"login_failed", b"login_ok")
assert hashlib.sha256(changed).hexdigest() != known_digest
print("evidence checks passed")
```

A matching digest supports byte identity relative to that recorded value. It cannot establish that the source system’s timestamp was accurate or that an attacker never altered the log before collection. Correlate independent sources and distinguish observed events from inferred causes. Normalize timestamps for comparison while preserving the original timestamp and its uncertainty.

Maintain a handling log recording each transfer, copy and analysis action with accountable identity and purpose. Protect sensitive content, restrict access and retain only under the applicable investigation policy. Do not upload real incident artifacts into an unrelated tool for convenience. If the question requires specialist acquisition or legal procedures, escalate to the appropriate qualified process; this introductory exercise does not establish forensic certification or courtroom suitability.

A useful report states what artifact was examined, what was observed, what method produced the conclusion and what remains uncertain. “The copied log contains this line and its digest matches the recorded copy” is narrower and more defensible than “the user definitely performed the action.”
