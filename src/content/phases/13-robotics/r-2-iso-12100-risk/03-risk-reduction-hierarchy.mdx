## Why this matters

A warning label cannot physically prevent a hand from entering a trapping point. Starting with the hazard's source can remove reliance on a person's reaction or on a protective device. [HSE's review of machinery-safety standards](https://www.hse.gov.uk/research/assets/docs/rr1214.pdf) describes the ISO 12100 priority: inherently safe design, safeguarding and complementary measures, then information for use.

This is an iterative design process. Record the measures considered, those implemented, their verification, and the remaining risks. Do not treat an undocumented assumption or a familiar device as proof of adequate protection.

## Start with something familiar

If a shelf has a sharp projecting corner, rounding or relocating it removes the source of harm. Padding it provides another layer; a warning sign asks people to avoid it. These choices act at different points between the source and the person.

## The mental model

> First change the source of harm, then protect people from what remains, then communicate the residual risk. Reassess the whole application after each change; layers do not excuse an ineffective earlier decision.

## Step 1 — Inherently safe design measures

Inherently safe design eliminates the hazard or reduces its risk by changing the design itself, not by adding external safeguards.

ISO 12100 lists specific inherently safe design measures:

**Geometric and physical aspects**:

- Eliminate sharp edges, corners, and protrusions that constitute hazards
- Reduce the robot's reach so operators cannot be in the hazardous zone during autonomous motion (workspace limitation by design)
- Reduce payload to reduce kinetic energy at impact

**Technical knowledge in mechanical design**:

- Choose lightweight end-effectors to reduce effective mass at the TCP (lower kinetic energy at contact)
- Use materials that deform at contact (soft cladding on rigid structures)
- Design fixtures so that the robot cannot move into the operator's body region during normal operation (mechanical stops, not software limits)

Mechanical retention and fail-safe behavior may address gravity or stored-energy hazards, but adding a second actuator or a brake is not automatically an adequate safety measure. Analyze its failure modes and substantiate its performance in the application.

**Reduction of exposure**:

- Move the machine tending interaction point outside the robot's reach (longer conveyor sections, improved part-delivery design) so the operator never needs to be near the robot
- Increase automation of the interaction to reduce human presence time

**The key test**: is there a design change — to the robot, the end-effector, the cell layout, the fixture, the task definition — that would eliminate the hazard or reduce its risk to an acceptable level? If yes, that change must be implemented (or a documented reason for not implementing it must be given). Documenting "we could not find a design change" without actually looking is the finding pattern auditors find most often.

### Inherently safe design worked example

For a trapping point between a wrist and fixture, investigate whether the fixture geometry and access arrangement can eliminate the trap. Verify that the redesign does not create another accessible pinch point or compromise workpiece retention.

Reducing speed from 1.2 m/s to 0.3 m/s reduces translational kinetic energy to 1/16 of its original value **if effective mass is unchanged**. It does not establish harmless contact: trapping force, geometry, effective mass, stopping behavior, and failure conditions still matter. An ordinary programmed speed setting is not a validated safety-related speed limit.

Therefore this example assigns neither a new severity category nor a required PL. Those require an application-specific reassessment with evidence, not a speed-ratio calculation. Record what was measured, which assumptions remain, and what safeguarding is needed for the remaining hazard.

## Step 2 — Safeguarding and protective measures

Safeguarding places barriers or sensors between the human and the hazard. ISO 12100 distinguishes:

**Guards**:

- Fixed guards — physical barriers secured in position, normally requiring a tool for removal; perimeter fencing is one example.
- Movable guards — physically blocking but can be opened (hinged gates, sliding doors). Select interlocking and, where necessary, guard locking according to access and stopping-time risks.
- Adjustable guards — guards with position-adjustable elements (adjustable bar spacing) for different product sizes.
- Self-closing guards — guards returned to the closed position by their own weight or a spring.

**Protective devices**:

- Safety light curtains — optical barriers that detect when a beam is broken by entry into the hazard zone; trigger a protective stop.
- Area scanners (safety laser scanners) — LIDAR-based devices that define two-dimensional safety zones; trigger speed reduction or stop on zone violation.
- Safety mats — pressure-sensitive mats that signal presence when stepped on.
- Enabling devices — three-position controls whose enabling position permits a separate motion command; squeezing fully or releasing removes the enable. They are not a substitute for all other protective measures.
- Two-hand controls — both hands must operate simultaneously, preventing them from being in the hazard zone during the dangerous phase.

**Positioning matters.** A presence-sensing device must detect access early enough for the protective response to be effective. [ISO 13855:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/80590.html) addresses safeguard positioning relative to human approach. ISO 13857 addresses reach distances; it is not interchangeable with a stopping-time calculation. Use the applicable approach geometry, response-time evidence, detection capability, and installation requirements rather than one universal formula.

### Select for the actual hazard

A physical guard may also contain ejected material that a light curtain cannot stop. A sensing device may suit frequent access only when the machine can reach a safe state before exposure and all access routes are addressed. Whole-body entry introduces further issues such as undetected presence and unexpected restart. Maintenance can require energy isolation beyond an interlocked stop. Selection follows the risk assessment; there is no general “3/3 rule.”

## Step 3 — Information for use

Information for use includes warnings, labels, operating manuals, training requirements, and personal protective equipment (PPE) requirements.

**Critical point**: ISO 12100 treats information for use as the last resort, not the first. A label saying "DANGER: Crush Hazard" on a machine with an unguarded pinch point does NOT substitute for a guard. The label is Step 3 for the residual risk that REMAINS after Steps 1 and 2 have been exhausted.

The types of information for use:

- **Warnings** — visual, audible, or tactile signals that alert to the presence of residual hazard (yellow/black warning labels, audible alarm during operation).
- **Operational instructions** — how to use the machine safely (operator manual, training protocol, safe work procedure).
- **Personal protective equipment requirements** — required PPE for the remaining hazard (cut-resistant gloves, safety glasses, steel-toed boots).
- **Commissioning / maintenance instructions** — how to safely access the machine for maintenance, including lockout-tagout procedures.

## Residual risk register

After the three-step hierarchy has been applied, some residual risk remains. ISO 12100 does not require residual risk to be zero — the objective is adequate risk reduction in accordance with applicable requirements. ALARP is a jurisdiction-specific decision framework and should not be presented as a universal ISO acceptance formula. The residual risk register documents what remains and why.

A useful entry records the hazard and task, measures considered, selected controls, verification evidence, remaining exposure, and required information for use. It also identifies unresolved assumptions and the person responsible for closing them. Do not mark a risk acceptable merely because further reduction affects cycle time or because a numerical score is low.

For a fixture redesign, the entry could say: “H-04: hand trapping during jam clearing; redesigned access geometry; verification of accessible gaps and workpiece retention pending; residual risk evaluation remains open; commissioning release depends on closing these checks and validating additional protective functions.” This is honest traceability, not a declaration of compliance.

- Apply design, safeguarding, and information for use in priority order and record the evidence behind each decision.
