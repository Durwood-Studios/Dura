## Why this matters

A motor torque setting does not uniquely determine the force or pressure at every possible contact. Robot configuration, tool shape, payload, speed, contact direction, trapping and response dynamics all matter. A PFL argument needs both the physical contact analysis and evidence for any safety-related functions relied upon.

## Start with something familiar

A spring-loaded door with a force setting can still hurt differently at its broad face and at the hinge gap. The setting, geometry and trapped body part interact. Robot contact similarly cannot be judged from one joint-torque number.

## The mental model

> Contact geometry and energy determine the physical challenge; sensing/estimation and protective response address specified behavior. Product evidence covers defined capabilities and conditions; the actual tool and task still need application assessment.

## Mechanical energy is part of the problem

For a simple ideal gear model, motor inertia reflected to the output scales with the square of the speed ratio. Real robot contact involves several links and actuators, configuration-dependent effective mass, structural compliance and control behavior. A gear ratio alone does not establish a particular contact-force peak or prove that every nonzero speed is unacceptable.

Using the elementary translational model E=½mv², a teaching mass of 4 kg moving at 0.5 m/s has 0.5 joule of kinetic energy. At 0.25 m/s it has 0.125 joule. This demonstrates quadratic speed sensitivity, not a safe energy limit for a person. The way energy transfers through a contact and whether a person can recoil matter; trapping can produce sustained force after the initial impact.

Low inertia, suitable contours, compliance, reduced motion and avoiding trapping can improve a design. None should be assumed from the word “cobot” or from an unsupported claim about a particular manufacturer's gearbox.

## Sensing, estimation and protective response

A robot may use dedicated torque/force sensing, other measurements with estimation, inherent design, or a combination supported by its architecture. It is not generally correct to require a specific output sensor on every joint or to assume motor-current estimation is inherently disallowed. Review the manufacturer's actual safety functions, limits, accuracy and fault response.

For an active protective function, trace:

```text
measured/estimated state → evaluation against configured conditions
→ protective command → physical response and final condition
```

Normal control-loop rate is not the same as safety-function reaction time or full stopping time. A 1 kHz controller does not automatically stop a loaded arm in 1 ms. Identify filtering, decision latency, actuation and motion after detection from the supported evidence.

Likewise, an ordinary application torque limit is not automatically a safety-rated protective function. If the safety case relies on it, substantiate the complete function's behavior and required integrity. Do not write a generic contact state machine that automatically resumes motion after N clear cycles and assume every application permits that restart.

## Configuration and contact-location effects

Different poses can turn the same joint torques into different Cartesian forces. Near certain configurations, unfavorable mechanical advantage or trapping can matter even at low speed. [Universal Robots' safety-function documentation](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW10_6/Content/prod-usr-man/hardware/arm_e-Series/UR16e/H_g5_sections/safetyFunctionsAndinterfaces/configurable_functions.htm) explicitly describes model-specific force-limiting exceptions and accuracies. Use the correct robot and software manual rather than transferring those values to another system.

Evaluate the entire possible contact envelope: robot links, tool, workpiece and fixtures. The average-pressure estimate F/A is useful for understanding why a smaller area is concerning, but it does not replace measurement of local peak pressure or appropriate testing of sharp contacts.

For example, the same 40 N spread uniformly over 4 cm² would average 10 N/cm², while over 0.5 cm² it would average 80 N/cm². Neither is an acceptance criterion. Actual area and pressure distribution can change during contact, and a protruding pin may require eliminating contact rather than attempting to approve it through a blunt-contact model.

## Read product evidence by scope

Obtain the actual declaration/certificate, safety manual, supported hardware/software versions and specified functions. Determine what is evaluated, under which operating conditions, and what the integrator must verify. Do not invent a generic “certified at 1,000 mm/s with 5 kg” envelope or assume every certificate covers contact at a custom tool.

[DGUV's PFL application guidance](https://www.dguv.de/medien/fb-holzundmetall/publikationen-dokumente/infoblaetter/infobl_englisch/080_collaborativerobotsystems.pdf) considers the robot, tool, workpiece and surrounding application together. [A3's measurement-method report overview](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/ria-standards-committee-releases-new-technical-report-on-testing-power-and-force-in-collaborative-robots) distinguishes quasi-static and transient measurement concerns. These support application-specific evaluation, not a blanket promise of harmless contact.

## What you learned

- Explain why an ordinary torque limit alone does not substantiate a PFL application.
- Connect mechanical energy, contact geometry, sensing/estimation and protective response.
- Trace the contact-response path without equating controller frequency with stopping time.
- Distinguish product evidence from the application-specific contact and integration validation it does not supply.
