## Why this matters

A scanner, controller and drive may each have safety ratings, yet their combination can fail to provide the required protective function. Wiring, configuration, timing, common causes and software all matter. You need to distinguish the performance demanded by the application from evidence that the complete implementation achieves it.

## Start with something familiar

A bridge design specifies the load it must carry. A material certificate tells you something about one steel component, but does not prove the bridge's joints, geometry and construction are adequate. A safety-function target and a component safety rating have the same distinction.

## The mental model

> Requirement → complete function → evidence. Reliability numbers describe one part of the evidence; architecture, systematic measures and validation determine whether the claim is justified.

## Functional safety and its scope

Functional safety concerns safety that depends on a system responding correctly to its inputs. For example, a protective function must detect entry and bring hazardous motion to the specified safe state within the required time. Its specification needs triggering conditions, response, timing, operating modes and failure behavior.

IEC 61508 provides a general framework for electrical/electronic/programmable electronic safety-related systems. Machinery standards adapt functional-safety methods to machine control. Other sectors have their own standards and terminology: do not convert medical software classes, automotive ASILs and machinery PLs as if they were interchangeable scales.

[ISO 13849-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html) addresses safety-related control parts, including software, for high-demand and continuous operation across electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic and mechanical technologies. It does not provide the required PL for every particular application.

[IEC 62061:2021](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/59927) addresses machinery safety-related control systems in high/continuous demand operation. Its 2021 edition extends to non-electrical technologies. Designing complex programmable electronic subsystems themselves is outside its scope; integrating appropriate subsystems and developing application software must follow the applicable requirements. “Mechanical means ISO, electronic means IEC” is therefore an unreliable selection rule.

## Required performance and achieved performance

The risk assessment and applicable machine-specific requirements determine the required safety performance. In ISO 13849, PL_r denotes the required performance level. The achieved PL must be substantiated for the implemented function.

For ISO 13849, relevant evidence includes category/architecture, component dangerous-failure data, diagnostic coverage, common-cause measures, systematic failures, software and validation. A simplified numerical method can assist evaluation under its assumptions; it is not a substitute for the rest of the requirements. Two channels alone do not prove Category 4 or PL e.

IEC 62061 expresses machinery safety integrity using SIL 1–3 and subsystem evidence. The achieved function is constrained by architectural and systematic requirements as well as its dangerous-failure frequency. A supplier's maximum SIL or PL capability describes conditions under which a subsystem can be used; integration must respect those conditions.

## Compare the numbers without relabeling the claim

For high-demand/continuous operation, compare dangerous-failure frequency per hour (PFH or PFH_D notation). Low-demand PFD_avg is a different quantity and cannot be inserted into a per-hour calculation.

The following is a **numerical comparison only**, consistent with the [IFA functional-safety report](https://publikationen.dguv.de/widgets/pdf/download/article/4150):

| PL band | Dangerous failures per hour | Numerically overlapping SIL band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | at least 10⁻⁵, below 10⁻⁴ | None of machinery SIL 1–3 |
| b | at least 3 × 10⁻⁶, below 10⁻⁵ | Within SIL 1's numerical interval; this does not qualify a PL b design for SIL 1 |
| c | at least 10⁻⁶, below 3 × 10⁻⁶ | Within SIL 1's numerical interval |
| d | at least 10⁻⁷, below 10⁻⁶ | SIL 2 numerical interval |
| e | at least 10⁻⁸, below 10⁻⁷ | SIL 3 numerical interval |

SIL 1's numerical interval runs from 10⁻⁶ up to, but not including, 10⁻⁵ per hour. It spans more than the PL c interval. The intervals themselves have defined boundaries; what is invalid is treating overlap as complete architectural or systematic equivalence.

Suppose a function's substantiated PFH_D is 5 × 10⁻⁷/h. The number lies in the PL d and SIL 2 intervals. It does not, by itself, prove either complete claim. You still need the relevant architecture, diagnostic and common-cause evidence, software/systematic measures, integration assumptions and validation.

## Selecting and combining evidence

Choose the governing method from the machine/application requirements, the technologies and operating mode in scope, available subsystem evidence and the team's competence. Document the decision and the edition used. A process-industry low-demand function requires its applicable sector method; do not select a machinery standard merely because the numerical label looks familiar.

When integrating a subsystem evaluated under another framework, use the applicable standard's integration provisions and the supplier's documented constraints. Such integration is not automatically prohibited, and it is not automatic equivalence. Trace what evidence transfers, what assumptions apply, and what remains to be evaluated for the complete function.

For a customer requesting SIL 2 where the existing file claims PL d, first clarify the specified standard and scope. Perform and document the required gap evaluation and additional verification; do not simply rename the certificate or assume a complete redesign is always necessary.

## What you learned

- Explain why machinery functional-safety methods share concerns while retaining their own scope and evidence requirements.
- Compare PL and SIL numerical intervals without relabeling a complete claim.
- Select an applicable framework from the application and evidence, not a mechanical/electronic stereotype.
- Analyze missing integration, software and validation evidence behind a proposed safety rating.
