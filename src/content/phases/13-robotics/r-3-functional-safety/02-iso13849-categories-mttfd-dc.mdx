## Why this matters

Two switches do not automatically make a redundant safety function. If both depend on one output element that can fail dangerously, a single fault may defeat the function. Review the complete sensor-to-actuator path and the evidence for each claimed diagnostic measure.

## Start with something familiar

Two backup pumps supplied through the same failed power cable cannot provide independent protection against that cable failure. Likewise, redundant control channels need an analysis of shared failures, not just a count of components.

## The mental model

> Draw the paths that must work, mark the faults that can defeat them, and mark which faults are detected in time. Combine that architecture with credible failure data; then check the remaining systematic and validation requirements.

## Categories describe fault behavior

Under [ISO 13849-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html), category is one part of the safety-related control design. These summaries orient the review; the complete category requirements and application assumptions still have to be checked.

| Category | Main distinction for review |
| --- | --- |
| B | Basic safety principles and suitable design; a fault can cause loss of the function. Ordinary components do not automatically satisfy even this baseline. |
| 1 | Adds well-tried components and safety principles; faults can still cause loss of the function. A large catalogue MTTFd alone does not establish “well-tried.” |
| 2 | Tests the safety function at suitable intervals and provides the required response to detected faults. The test path and timing are part of the analysis. |
| 3 | A single fault must not cause loss of the safety function; detect faults where reasonably practicable. Accumulated undetected faults can matter. |
| 4 | Single faults must not cause loss, with stronger detection and fault-accumulation requirements. Two channels or continuous comparison alone are not sufficient evidence. |

Common-cause measures matter in the designated Category 2, 3 and 4 approaches. A test unit can share vulnerabilities with the functional channel too. For a selected Annex F assessment, substantiate the applicable measures and required score; do not assume a different manufacturer's name proves independence.

For Category 2, do not reverse the relationship between testing and demands: a test intended to find latent faults generally needs to occur sufficiently often relative to demands, or satisfy the applicable alternative timing conditions. The classic simplified model assumes a test **frequency** at least 100 times the demand frequency, not a test interval 100 times longer. Use the selected edition's conditions and the actual fault response; [IFA's simplified-model discussion](https://www.dguv.de/medien/ifa/en/pub/rep/pdf/rep07/biar0208/rep22008e.pdf) explains why the assumptions matter.

Category is not synonymous with PL. In particular, “Category 3 can never achieve PL e” is false: [DGUV's certification-mark categories](https://presse.dguv.de/dguv-test/prod-pruef-zert/pruefzeichen/dguv-test-zeichen/zeichenzusaetze/index.jsp) explicitly include Category 3 / PL e. The complete evidence determines what is supportable.

## MTTFd: use the right failure data

Mean time to dangerous failure is a reliability-model parameter, not a guaranteed service life or a replacement interval. Record its source and operating conditions. Depending on the component, data may be supplied as MTTFd, B10d with an operating-cycle model, a subsystem PFH_D, or other justified data under the selected method. Lack of a B10d field does not automatically disqualify an electronic subsystem that supplies appropriate PFH_D evidence.

The usual channel classifications are Low (3 to below 10 years), Medium (10 to below 30), and High (30 to 100). Calculation limits depend on category and method; Category 4 can use values above 100 under the relevant conditions. [IFA's calculator guidance](https://dguv.de/ifa%3B/praxishilfen/practical-solutions-machine-safety/performance-level-calculator/index.jsp) explicitly distinguishes that case.

For an illustrative channel of independent components whose dangerous failure rates add, use reciprocal addition, with consistent units:

```text
1 / MTTFd_channel = 1 / MTTFd_1 + 1 / MTTFd_2
```

For components with 60 and 120 years, the uncapped channel result is 40 years. Taking only the weaker component's 60 years would overestimate channel reliability. Apply the method's component and channel restrictions at the prescribed stages; this arithmetic is not a complete PL calculation.

## DC: weight by dangerous failures

Diagnostic coverage is the fraction of dangerous failures detected by the diagnostics. It concerns failure rates, not simply the percentage of code tested or sensors polled. A rapid comparison does not detect faults outside its diagnostic mechanism.

Where the weighted averaging model applies:

```text
DCavg = (DC1 / MTTFd1 + DC2 / MTTFd2) / (1 / MTTFd1 + 1 / MTTFd2)
```

Using DC1=90%, MTTFd1=60 years, DC2=60%, MTTFd2=120 years gives 80%. The less reliable component contributes more to the result. The ordinary classification intervals are None below 60%, Low from 60% to below 90%, Medium from 90% to below 99%, and High at least 99%.

Each diagnostic claim needs a failure-mode rationale and applicable assumptions. Do not assign “High” merely because the software polls continuously. Record what the test detects, when detection occurs, the reaction, and failures it misses.

## From calculations to a supported PL

For a selected simplified method, establish architecture, MTTFd, DCavg and common-cause measures before using its tables or a suitable calculation tool. The resulting numerical assessment still depends on the model assumptions. Document systematic measures, software, mission/use constraints, integration and validation of the full function. Compare the achieved PL with the independently determined PL_r; there is no “PLr-achieved” quantity.

## What you learned

- Identify the complete function's fault-tolerance and testing evidence before accepting a category label.
- Interpret dangerous-failure data with its operating assumptions and category-specific calculation limits.
- Calculate reciprocal channel MTTFd and failure-rate-weighted diagnostic coverage under an explicit model.
- Reject unsupported category and PL claims while preserving the distinction between required and achieved performance.
