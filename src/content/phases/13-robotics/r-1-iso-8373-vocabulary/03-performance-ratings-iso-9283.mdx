## Why this matters

A robot may repeatedly arrive at nearly the same point while that point is offset from the command. A small repeatability number therefore cannot establish that a CAD-derived target will be reached accurately. It also says little about a moving tool's entire path.

Imagine repeatedly throwing darts into a tight group away from the bullseye. The group illustrates consistency; its offset illustrates a different error. Moving the target does not tell you whether the next group will remain tight.

> Separate scatter, systematic offset, and path behavior. Compare each required performance quantity under documented conditions instead of treating one small number as a universal precision guarantee.

## Accuracy and repeatability

Conceptually, pose accuracy concerns the difference between a commanded pose and the mean attained pose. Pose repeatability concerns the spread of repeated attained poses under the test conditions. Pose includes orientation as well as position; a quoted millimeter value does not describe every orientation characteristic.

[ISO 9283:1998](https://www.iso.org/standard/22244.html) specifies performance criteria and related test methods for industrial manipulating robots. Use its actual definitions and calculations for a formal report. This lesson's small examples explain the distinction; they do not implement the complete standard's repeatability statistic.

Accuracy is not defined by whether the target was taught or typed. Teaching can compensate for a repeatable local offset, which makes repeatability relevant to repeated pick tasks. Offline coordinates can expose errors in robot, tool, and work-object calibration. Both kinds of application can require accuracy and repeatability evidence.

There is no universal ratio saying accuracy error must be ten to fifty times repeatability. Calibration may improve systematic errors, but it does not guarantee a particular result or eliminate tool deflection, thermal effects, fixture error, and measurement uncertainty.

## Worked example: one-dimensional teaching data

Commanded position is 100.00 mm. Five measured positions are 100.98, 101.00, 101.02, 101.00, and 101.00 mm. Their mean is 101.00 mm, so the mean offset is +1.00 mm. The observed range is 0.04 mm.

These data show a tight group with a large offset relative to a fictional ±0.10 mm target tolerance. The range is an elementary descriptive measure, **not** an ISO 9283 repeatability result. More repeats cannot by themselves remove the systematic offset.

Before attributing the offset to the robot, check the coordinate frames, tool center point, measurement reference, and setup. The observed error could include these contributions.

## Match conditions before comparing numbers

Request the relevant test report or documented specification: quantity, method and edition, test positions and poses, approaches, load and inertia, speed, warm-up and environment, measurement system, and uncertainty. Do not assume missing conditions mean either rated load or an optimistic center-of-workspace test.

A second vendor's more detailed conditions make the number easier to interpret, but do not prove it is universally more conservative. Load, configuration, approach, and thermal state can matter; the relationship must be measured or justified rather than assumed monotonic for every robot.

## Path performance is a separate requirement

A maximum TCP speed is a motion capability under stated conditions, not a path-error tolerance. Endpoint repeatability does not establish accuracy along an arc or during continuous dispensing. Investigate actual path accuracy and repeatability at the required speed, curvature, load, and process conditions.

If a weld appears wavy, possible contributors include tracking, process settings, fixtures, and vibration. A controlled speed comparison can test a hypothesis; it does not justify diagnosing the servo system from a photograph alone. Validate the resulting process against its requirements.

## What you learned

- Distinguish repeated scatter from error relative to a commanded pose.
- A small repeatability number cannot establish absolute-position or path suitability.
- Compare documented methods and conditions without guessing missing information.
