## Why this matters

A gripper can introduce a sharp edge or trap a hand even when its supporting arm has protective capabilities. Precise vocabulary helps you describe which part moves, which part contacts the work, and which application needs evaluation.

Think of a robot arm holding a paintbrush. The arm is the moving mechanism; the brush is the task tool. Saying “the robot is safe” leaves both the tool and the surrounding task unspecified.

> Describe the mechanism, tool, pose, and application separately. A component's capability is evidence to evaluate within an application, not a conclusion about every possible use.

## Mechanism and task tool

In this introductory description, the **manipulator** is the linked and jointed mechanism that moves and positions objects or tools. The **end-effector** performs the intended task at its working end, such as a gripper or welding torch. A sensor may be part of a tool assembly; the mere presence of a sensor does not make every sensor an end-effector.

A robot application includes more than the arm and tool: the workpiece, controller, fixtures, surrounding equipment, people, and operating tasks matter. Use these descriptions to distinguish boundaries; consult [ISO 8373:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/75539.html) for controlled terminology rather than treating this lesson's paraphrases as verbatim definitions.

## Position, orientation, and reach

**Position** describes where a reference point is. **Orientation** describes how the object is turned. A **pose** combines both. In three-dimensional space, a free rigid body has three translational and three rotational degrees of freedom; this does not mean every robot can independently achieve every such motion.

Two grippers can have the same reference-point position while one points upward and the other sideways. Their poses differ, and they may contact different objects.

Reachable positions do not imply all orientations are attainable at each position. A workspace drawing must be interpreted with its stated reference point, tool, joint constraints, and other conditions. It is not a drawing of the validated safe area, nor does it establish required clearance from people.

## Collaborative operation is an application question

You will encounter protective approaches including monitored stopping, hand guiding, speed and separation monitoring, and power and force limiting. Their names describe different ways to control interaction hazards. They may be combined where the application supports a coherent protective design.

A product labeled collaborative does not establish that its tool, payload, contact geometry, or surrounding cell is acceptable. [A3's overview of the updated ISO 10218 standards](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/updated-iso-10218-major-advancements-in-industrial-robot-safety-standards-now-available) distinguishes robot requirements from application and integration requirements. Later lessons examine these protective approaches and their evidence; this lesson only establishes the distinction between a capability and an application conclusion.

## Worked example

A six-joint arm holds a welding torch beside a fixture. The arm is the manipulator, and the torch is its task end-effector. A command that moves the torch tip to a point but omits orientation does not fully specify the desired pose. Calling the arm collaborative says nothing by itself about exposure to the welding process or trapping against the fixture.

## Guided practice

A robot carries a gripper to the same point twice, first with its fingers horizontal and then vertical. Name what stayed the same, what changed, and one application detail still needed before evaluating interaction hazards.

The chosen reference point's position stayed the same. Orientation changed, so the pose changed. Relevant missing application details include the carried object, accessible tool edges, fixtures, or how a person could approach the motion. The same position does not imply the same contact scenario.

## What you learned

- Distinguish the manipulator from the end-effector and the complete application.
- A pose includes position and orientation; reach does not guarantee every orientation.
- A collaborative label does not validate a tool, task, or cell.
