## Why this matters

A short datasheet helps screen products. It usually cannot establish every condition needed to integrate one into an application. Reading it well means distinguishing a stated value, its conditions, and the conclusion someone hopes to draw from it.

Treat a datasheet as a map legend rather than a completed journey. It explains advertised features, but the actual route still depends on the terrain and your destination.

> Record what the supplier actually states, then request the missing evidence needed for your application. Do not translate a marketing name into a protective function by guessing.

## A fictional datasheet

The following is an invented teaching example, not a specification or allegation about any actual vendor:

| Field | Advertised text |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Example Arm A |
| Payload | 10 kg |
| Reach | 1300 mm |
| Axes | 6 |
| Repeatability | 0.03 mm |
| TCP speed | 1.5 m/s maximum |
| Protective capability | STO; speed monitoring; enabling device |
| Marketing | No cage needed; smooth surfaces eliminate pinch risk |
| Operator modes | Meet-and-assist; inspection |
| Claimed standard | ISO 10218-1:2025 |

Suppose the task uses an offset gripper to place a part beside a fixed fixture while an operator loads another station. The tool, workpiece, layout, access, and recovery tasks are not specified by the table.

## Worked example: separate observation and inference

**Observation:** the table states a 10 kg payload. **Evidence request:** obtain the supported total tool/workpiece load conditions, center-of-gravity and inertia limits, mounting conditions, and relevant load diagrams. Do not assume that every robot expresses this as a payload-versus-reach curve.

**Observation:** repeatability is given as 0.03 mm. **Evidence request:** identify the measured quantity, method, conditions, and appropriate path or accuracy evidence for the task. A small repeatability value does not establish absolute positioning.

**Observation:** six axes are stated. **Evidence request:** obtain the joint arrangement and actual pose/path envelope. The count does not alone establish a particular architecture or arbitrary tool orientation.

[ISO 8373:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/75539.html) supplies robotics terminology; [ISO 9283:1998](https://www.iso.org/standard/22244.html) concerns performance criteria and test methods. Neither turns an incomplete marketing table into application validation.

## Interpret protective capabilities by their actual boundaries

“STO” identifies a drive-related safe torque off capability, but its availability does not prove the whole machine's emergency-stop performance, mechanical holding, or isolation for maintenance. Ask for the function description, supported configuration, certificate scope, integration requirements, and stopping behavior relevant to the application.

Likewise, “inspection” does not tell you whether the robot stops, remains moving under monitored separation, or uses another protective arrangement. Request the documented mode behavior and safety-function configuration. Do not map a vendor label to a standardized approach without evidence.

An ISO 10218-1 claim concerns the robot scope. The application and integration require separate consideration; [A3's updated-standard overview](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/updated-iso-10218-major-advancements-in-industrial-robot-safety-standards-now-available) explains the distinction between robot and application requirements. Obtain the relevant declaration or evidence instead of declaring the claim valid from its presence in a table.

## Replace blanket claims with questions

“No cage needed” requires asking what access and protective design are supported in the actual application. It is not a universal permission to omit guarding. “Smooth surfaces eliminate pinch risk” ignores possible trapping between the robot, tool, workpiece, and fixture.

For the example task, useful questions include: which trapping geometries are foreseeable, what protects the loading operator during motion, how are setup and recovery handled, and what evidence validates the selected protective measures? These are application questions rather than five universal mandatory datasheet fields.

## Guided practice

The supplier says its “meet-and-assist” mode automatically makes the shared area safe. Write a precise request that avoids assuming which protective approach is implemented.

<details>
<summary>Compare the request</summary>

Please provide the documented motion and access behavior for meet-and-assist, its protective functions and boundaries, required external devices and configuration, and the validation/integration evidence for the proposed task. Include transitions into and out of the mode and behavior under relevant faults. The name alone does not identify a monitored stop, hand-guiding, separation-monitoring, or contact-limiting arrangement.

</details>

## What you learned

- Extract stated quantities without silently adding missing conditions.
- Turn marketing claims into precise questions about function and application boundaries.
- Request evidence matched to the actual tool, load, motion, people, and tasks.
