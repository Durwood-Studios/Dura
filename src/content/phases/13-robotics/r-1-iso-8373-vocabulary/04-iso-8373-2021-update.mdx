## Why this matters

A revised vocabulary can change how a document describes a system. It does not follow that every older document uses a deprecated term, or that changing a label fixes an engineering problem. Start with what the revision actually says.

Think of a vocabulary edition as a dictionary edition in a shared library. You need to know which edition a writer used before deciding whether an entry changed. A new book on the shelf does not silently rewrite every existing reference.

> Record the edition and the evidence for each terminology change. A vocabulary change and an application safety change are separate findings.

## Verified revision facts

[ISO's catalog](https://www.iso.org/standard/75539.html) identifies ISO 8373:2021 as the third edition, published in November 2021, following the 2012 edition. It concerns robotics terminology.

The standard's [foreword in the published preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/75539/1bc8409322eb4922bf680e15901852d2/ISO-8373-2021.pdf) describes reviewed definitions, additions including medical robots, wearable robots and modularity, and alignment with related standards. These are the verified revision points to remember; they do not establish a list of deprecated words.

A claimed deletion or replacement requires comparing the actual relevant entries. Do not infer that “industrial robot” was removed merely because robot applications have diversified. Nor should a marketing term be treated as a defined structural class without checking the vocabulary.

## Keep reference and application questions separate

For a terminology review, retain the source document identifier and edition, the old expression in context, the corresponding controlled entry, and the proposed wording with its reason. If you cannot verify the claimed change, record it as unresolved instead of inventing a replacement.

For a safety review, identify the applicable application requirements and actual protective evidence. A vocabulary standard does not itself validate a mobile robot, wearable device, or collaborative cell. Merely replacing “cobot” with another phrase cannot establish that hazardous contact has been addressed.

Do not assume all references automatically adopt every later edition. Check whether the source cites a particular edition and how the applicable document handles references. Preserve this distinction in the review record.

## Worked example

A 2019 report calls a product a “cobot” and states that no application evaluation is needed. The terminology review should identify what the report intended and which controlled terms apply. Separately, the unsupported claim that a label eliminates application evaluation needs engineering review. Correct spelling or updated terminology does not supply the missing evidence.

Now suppose a report simply uses an older term for a mechanism and provides a clear definition. You still compare the entries, but the age of the word alone is not proof of a safety defect.
