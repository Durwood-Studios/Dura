## Why this matters

A report can contain accurate measurements and still reach an invalid conclusion if it uses the wrong criterion. A finger trapped against a fixture and an unrestrained arm struck in open space have different contact conditions. A head-contact scenario cannot be made acceptable by borrowing a number from a table.

Think of criterion selection as choosing a measuring scale: reading the scale precisely is useless if it measures the wrong thing. Here, the scale also has an edition, a scope, and conditions that must match the application.

> Start with the foreseeable contact geometry, then identify applicable criteria and a measurement method. A numerical comparison supports a conclusion only within those documented conditions.

## Classify the contact before selecting criteria

**Quasi-static contact** includes trapping or clamping: the body part cannot move away from the applied load. A robot pressing a hand against a fixed fixture is a clear example. **Transient contact** involves an impact in which the body part can deflect; a short commanded movement alone does not demonstrate this condition.

Inspect the entire relevant trajectory and nearby structures. An initially free impact can become constrained if the moving body part meets an obstruction. Include foreseeable recovery and setup tasks; an operator's intention to pull away does not remove a trapping geometry.

Do not infer contact type from distraction alone. A distracted operator may face additional risk, but constrained versus unrestrained contact requires evidence about movement and surroundings.

Sensitive regions require prevention decisions before numerical testing. DGUV guidance excludes foreseeable head and neck contact through layout and protective measures; it does not justify such contact by assigning a convenient transient limit. See [DGUV collaborative robot guidance](https://www.dguv.de/medien/fb-holzundmetall/publikationen-dokumente/infoblaetter/infobl_englisch/080_collaborativerobotsystems.pdf).

## Record the criterion rather than memorize a table

For each retained test scenario, document:

- The actual body region and contact geometry, including any possible restraint.
- The applicable publication, edition, clause or table, and conditions of use.
- Each required quantity and its units, along with the selected measurement method.
- Restrictions, exclusions, and uncertainties that could invalidate the comparison.

Use the applicable robot application requirements and controlled standards copies. [ISO 10218-2:2025](https://www.iso.org/standard/73934.html?browse=tc) concerns industrial robot applications and cells. Do not assume that a table labeled “Annex A” in another publication is an annex of that standard, or that every body-region entry supplies four permissible values.

## Worked example: force and pressure are different evidence

The following numbers are **fictional exercise criteria**, not human-contact limits: force must be at most 100 N, and pressure at most 200 N/cm². Assume a circular flat tip has diameter 4 mm and carries 40 N uniformly.

1. Radius is 2 mm, or 0.2 cm.
2. Area is π × 0.2² ≈ 0.126 cm².
3. Average pressure is 40 / 0.126 ≈ 318 N/cm².
4. The force comparison meets the supplied exercise criterion, but average pressure exceeds its supplied criterion.

The arithmetic explains why force alone cannot answer a pressure question. It does **not** predict a real contact's peak pressure: contact area changes with deformation, and edges can concentrate load. Even a low average cannot establish that the local peak is below a limit. Suitable measurements and the specified interpretation remain necessary.

## Guided practice

A learner labels a hand-against-fixture scenario “transient because the robot stops quickly.” The report gives 30 N and a nominal area of 0.3 cm², but no pressure measurement or source for its limits. Identify three problems before accepting any comparison.

<details>
<summary>Review the reasoning</summary>

The fixture can restrain the hand, so stopping quickly does not justify transient classification. The average-pressure calculation, 30 / 0.3 = 100 N/cm², does not establish peak pressure. Finally, the criteria need a source, edition, applicability conditions, and a measurement method; without these the report has no defensible acceptance basis.

</details>

## What you learned

- Classify contact using actual restraint and movement, including transitions into trapping.
- Force and average pressure do not substitute for required peak-pressure evidence.
- A criterion record needs a source, applicability conditions, units, and a suitable measurement method.
