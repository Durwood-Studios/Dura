## Reframing failures as design input

When a measurement exceeds an Annex A threshold, the instinct is to question the measurement. Before re-running anything, read the failure closely:

- Did **force** exceed the threshold but pressure pass? → The force limit is binding; the end-effector has a large enough contact area. Reduce the contact force — which means reducing speed or payload.
- Did **pressure** exceed the threshold but force pass? → The end-effector has a small contact area creating high pressure even at modest forces. Increasing the contact area (geometric change to the end-effector) will resolve this.
- Did **both** exceed? → A speed reduction will help, but if the pressure-to-force ratio is very high (small contact area), you need the geometric change first.

The failure mode tells you the lever. The analysis below gives you the math.

## Lever 1: Speed reduction for transient contact

For transient contact, the peak impact force scales approximately with the effective mass of the robot's end-effector at the contact point and the approach velocity. The relationship (derived from impulse-momentum theory for elastic contact) is:

> F_peak ≈ v_impact × √(k × m_eff)

Where:

- v_impact = velocity of the end-effector at the contact point (TCP linear velocity)
- k = stiffness of the contact (dominated by the tissue simulant / human tissue)
- m_eff = effective mass of the robot at the TCP in the contact direction

Since k and m_eff are fixed for a given robot configuration and end-effector:

> F_peak ∝ v_impact

To reduce F_peak from the measured value F_measured to the threshold F_limit:

> v_new = v_current × (F_limit / F_measured)

Example: measurement shows 95 N transient force at TCP speed 600 mm/s. Hand dorsal transient threshold is 160 N. The measurement is passing (95 N < 160 N). But consider the finger threshold: 40 N.

If the scenario also covers finger contact and the measurement was 52 N (exceeding 40 N):

> v_new = 600 mm/s × (40 N / 52 N) = 600 × 0.769 = 461 mm/s

Reduce the programmed TCP speed at that trajectory segment from 600 mm/s to 461 mm/s or below. Re-test at the new speed.

**Important caveat:** speed reduction affects cycle time. A 23% speed reduction on a pick-and-place cycle that runs 180 times/hour is a real production impact. Quantify the throughput loss and present it to the customer before implementing.

## Lever 2: Geometric change to reduce contact pressure

When pressure fails with force passing, the end-effector contact geometry is the variable. The relationship is:

> P = F / A_contact

To bring measured pressure P_measured below threshold P_limit with fixed force:

> A_required = F_measured / P_limit

Example: measurement shows 45 N force (passing 160 N limit) and 380 N/cm² pressure (exceeding 310 N/cm² limit) at a finger dorsal scenario. The contact is from a 3 mm diameter probe tip.

Current contact area: A = π × (1.5 mm)² = π × 0.0225 cm² ≈ 0.071 cm²
Required area: A_required = 45 N / 310 N/cm² ≈ 0.145 cm²
Required diameter: d = 2 × √(A/π) = 2 × √(0.145/π) ≈ 0.43 cm = 4.3 mm

Adding a 4.3 mm diameter compliant cap to the probe tip would resolve the pressure exceedance. However: a geometric change to the end-effector changes its certified configuration. Depending on the robot manufacturer and the certification pathway, adding a cap may require a new CE declaration for the end-effector. Check before implementing.

**Note on compliant end-effectors:** adding a compliant rubber or foam cover to the end-effector increases contact area and distributes force — reducing peak pressure. This is a valid remediation and is widely used. The material and durometer of the cover must be documented and must remain stable over the operational life of the cell.

## Lever 3: Trajectory restriction

If the failing scenario occurs when the robot's end-effector enters a specific zone (e.g., "end-effector above 1500 mm height"), a trajectory restriction can be implemented as a software safety function:

- Add a joint-space or Cartesian-space zone limit that prevents the end-effector from entering the at-risk zone while operating at full PFL speed.
- The zone limit must be implemented as a safety-rated function (PL c minimum, per ISO 13849) so it cannot be bypassed by trajectory errors.

The trajectory restriction effectively removes the at-risk contact scenario from the PFL operating envelope. It must then be documented: "Contact scenario M-04 (end-effector above 1500 mm / head contact) is excluded from PFL operation by a safety-rated Cartesian zone limit implemented per safety function SF-09."

The excluded zone becomes its own entry in the risk assessment: above 1500 mm, what collaborative mode applies? If the robot is now prevented from operating in PFL mode above that height, and the operator can still be at head height (e.g., inspecting the top of the cell), the risk assessment must address what happens above that zone.

## Lever 4: Hard guard for the residual scenario

Some contact scenarios cannot be brought within Annex A limits at production speed, with the required payload, with practical geometric changes. This is an honest residual: the scenario is physically impossible to PFL-validate as designed.

The option is a hard guard: a physical barrier that prevents operator body parts from entering the zone where the at-risk contact occurs. Once a hard guard is present, the zone is no longer a collaborative workspace — it's a guarded zone. The ISO 10218-2 cell layout must reflect this; the PFL mode does not apply in the guarded zone.

When a hard guard is installed:

- Remove the corresponding scenarios from the PFL test matrix — they're not PFL scenarios.
- Add the guard to the cell's ISO 12100 hazard register as a protective measure.
- Verify the guard rating: a guard that prevents head/hand entry must be rated for the robot's maximum speed and payload per ISO 14120 (guards for machinery).
- The residual risk register should note the guarded zone and the rationale.

## Re-test obligations by lever

| Lever applied                   | Re-test requirement                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Speed reduction                 | Re-test all affected matrix entries at the new speed                                                  |
| Geometric change (end-effector) | Re-test ALL matrix entries — the end-effector mass has changed, affecting every transient scenario    |
| Trajectory restriction (soft)   | Re-test the restricted scenarios to confirm exclusion works; verify the safety function per ISO 13849 |
| Hard guard                      | No re-test of guarded scenarios (they're no longer PFL); test the guard per ISO 14120                 |
| Compliant cover added           | Re-test all affected pressure-limited entries; mass change may affect force measurements              |
