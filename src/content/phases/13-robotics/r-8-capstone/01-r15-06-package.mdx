## Why this matters

A collection of safety terms is not a reviewable engineering argument. A useful package connects the application, each hazard, the selected measure, and the evidence needed to show that the measure works. Missing information must remain visible.

Think of the package as an evidence ledger. Each conclusion should point to supporting facts; a blank evidence field is a task to resolve rather than an invitation to invent a certificate, measurement, or numeric limit.

> Make the chain from hazard to measure to verification explicit, and label assumptions and unresolved work. A simulated package can demonstrate reasoning without pretending to validate a real machine.

## Scenario and boundaries

Use this fictional scenario: an industrial arm moves small boxed parts from a conveyor to a fixture. A person replenishes boxes at an adjacent loading station, clears jams, and performs end-of-shift cleaning. The robot has an electrically actuated gripper. An engineering laptop can change the application program.

Choose and document a layout and operating concept. No actual robot model, safety certificate, stop measurement, contact limit, or performance level is supplied. You must identify what evidence would be needed. You may use explicitly fictional values to illustrate a calculation, but must not present them as standards requirements or real measurements.

[ANSI/A3 R15.06-2025](https://www.automate.org/robotics/safety/robot-safety-standard-documents) is the named professional reference for this module. Use controlled applicable requirements in actual work; the educational rubric below is not the standard's complete conformance checklist.

## Build the review package

Start with use, space, time, people, operating states, and foreseeable intervention limits. Draw a layout and identify accessible motion, gripping, stored energy, conveyor interaction, and relevant nonmechanical hazards. Consider normal work, setup, jams, cleaning, and maintenance rather than only the production cycle.

For each scenario, record the hazard, exposed person/task, possible harm, existing evidence, risk-estimation assumptions, and evaluation. Select measures following risk-reduction principles. Some hazards are reduced by geometry or inherently safer design; not every hazard maps directly to a control-system safety function.

Where a protective function is required, describe the trigger, response, operating conditions, reset/restart behavior, failure response, required integrity basis, and verification plan. Separate required from achieved PL/SIL. Missing component and system evidence means achieved integrity remains unestablished; a list of component labels is insufficient.

Describe how protection continues through operating-state changes. Maintenance may require isolation and control of stored energy; a monitored stop is not automatically an isolation procedure. Do not choose a protective approach merely because it is commonly associated with a mode name.

For potential contacts, first identify prevention requirements and actual constraint geometry. If justified contact testing remains necessary, identify the applicable criterion source, test method, conditions, uncertainty rule, and required evidence. Do not populate a body-region table with remembered values or assume that every region has an acceptable transient-contact limit.

Finally, model program/configuration access, maintenance connections, authorization, and change validation. Identify responsibilities for evidence collection, review, maintenance, and unresolved items.

## Worked traceability example

Hazard H-02: during jam clearing, a hand can be trapped between the gripper and fixture. Proposed measure M-02: change the access/layout and establish an appropriate protected intervention procedure. If a control function is part of that design, link its requirements and verification separately.

Evidence E-02 should address the reachable gap, foreseeable intervention positions, relevant stored energy, and whether the protective measure remains effective through the procedure. Record missing dimensions and actual equipment behavior as unresolved. “Robot is collaborative” is not supporting evidence for H-02.

A traceability row can contain: H-02 → M-02 → function specification if applicable → verification plan E-02 → owner and status. Keep the hazard in the record after selecting a measure so a reviewer can follow its resolution.

## Guided practice

A draft lists “PLe safety controller” as the complete evidence for a protective stop, and calls a screenshot of a course completion certificate proof that the package is validated. Identify the two broken evidence links.

<details>
<summary>Review the missing links</summary>

The controller label does not establish the complete function's required or achieved integrity, integration, timing, response, or validation. A course completion record does not assess the submitted engineering package. Even a hash of a file would establish a content identifier, not technical correctness or authorship. Request the function-level evidence and an appropriate review of the actual package.

## What you learned

- Create a reviewable package with bounded assumptions and task-specific hazards.
- Trace measures and protective functions to verification evidence.
- Justify contact and security decisions without manufacturing conformance claims.
- Keep unresolved validation work visible and distinguish learning records from engineering approval.
