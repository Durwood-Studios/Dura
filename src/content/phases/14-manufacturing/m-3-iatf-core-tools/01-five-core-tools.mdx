## Where this lesson sits

M2 named the five IATF Core Tools as a list. M3 unpacks each one with enough depth that an engineer can produce the deliverables, not just recognize them. The end-of-lesson verify artifact is a complete PPAP package — hash-anchored via /verify so a hiring manager can audit it.

The Core Tools are the IATF QMS made operational. ISO 9001 says "you need a quality management system"; IATF 16949 says "you need a QMS plus these five named tools used in this order during production launch."

## The five Core Tools, in workflow order

The Core Tools are not a flat list of disconnected practices — they're a sequenced workflow that walks a new part from design intent to serial production. Reading them in workflow order makes the relationships obvious:

> **APQP** — Advanced Product Quality Planning. The umbrella process. Phases 1–5 walk the launch from initial concept to production validation.
> **FMEA** — Failure Mode and Effects Analysis. Applied at design (DFMEA) and process (PFMEA). Identifies failure modes, ranks them by severity × occurrence × detection (RPN), drives risk reduction.
> **MSA** — Measurement System Analysis. Validates that the measurement system can detect the variation you care about. The output is a gage R&R study.
> **SPC** — Statistical Process Control. Monitors the production process against control limits to detect when it drifts. Outputs Cpk and Ppk capability indices.
> **PPAP** — Production Part Approval Process. The deliverable the supplier submits to the customer to demonstrate the production line is ready. PPAP bundles outputs from APQP, FMEA, MSA, and SPC into a single auditable package.

A common misread of the order: putting PPAP first because it's the most visible deliverable. PPAP is last — it's the package that demonstrates everything else was done correctly. APQP is the umbrella process; the others are activities within it.

## APQP — the five phases

APQP, defined by AIAG's manual (current edition: 2nd edition, 2008, with ongoing updates), structures the production launch into five sequential phases:

1. **Plan and Define Program** — voice of customer, business case, design goals, reliability and quality goals.
2. **Product Design and Development** — DFMEA, design reviews, prototype build, drawings and specifications, material specifications.
3. **Process Design and Development** — PFMEA, process flow charts, control plan (prototype), packaging specifications.
4. **Product and Process Validation** — production trial run, MSA, preliminary process capability study, packaging evaluation, PPAP submission.
5. **Feedback, Assessment, and Corrective Action** — reduced variation, customer satisfaction, delivery and service, effective use of lessons learned.

Each phase has documented inputs and outputs. APQP is gate-driven: a phase doesn't close until its outputs are produced and reviewed. Engineers who treat APQP as bureaucratic overhead miss that the gates are how problems get caught upstream of production launch — when fixing them is still cheap.

## FMEA — Design vs Process

**FMEA** (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) is the systematic identification of how a product or process could fail, the consequences of each failure, and the controls in place to prevent or detect the failure. There are two types, applied at different points in APQP:

### DFMEA — Design FMEA

Produced during APQP Phase 2 (Product Design and Development). Examines how the PRODUCT could fail: which design choices could result in failure modes, what the consequence of each failure is, what design controls are in place to prevent or detect each.

The DFMEA scores each failure mode on:

- **Severity (S)** — how bad is the consequence if this failure occurs.
- **Occurrence (O)** — how likely is this failure to occur given current design controls.
- **Detection (D)** — how likely are current design controls to detect this failure before reaching the customer.

The Risk Priority Number (RPN) = S × O × D. High-RPN failure modes drive design changes. The AIAG-VDA harmonized FMEA handbook (2019) introduced Action Priority (AP) as a complement to RPN; both are in active use as of 2026.

### PFMEA — Process FMEA

Produced during APQP Phase 3 (Process Design and Development). Examines how the MANUFACTURING PROCESS could fail: which process steps could go wrong, what the consequence is, what process controls (inspection, poka-yoke, SPC charts) are in place.

The PFMEA is the parent of the Control Plan. Every failure mode in the PFMEA that is not eliminated by design must have a corresponding process control in the Control Plan. Auditors trace from PFMEA failure modes to Control Plan entries; gaps are non-conformances.

## MSA — Why measurement comes before SPC

MSA (Measurement System Analysis) validates that the measurement system itself is capable of detecting the variation you care about. If your measurement device, operator, and procedure together produce more variation than the part you're measuring, your SPC charts will detect measurement noise, not process variation. SPC is meaningless without MSA first.

The standard MSA output is a **gage R&R study** (Repeatability and Reproducibility):

- **Repeatability** — variation when the same operator measures the same part multiple times with the same gage.
- **Reproducibility** — variation when different operators measure the same part with the same gage.

Combined gage R&R is expressed as a % of the tolerance or % of the total variation. AIAG's rough guidance: < 10% is acceptable, 10–30% is marginal, > 30% requires improving the measurement system before relying on SPC.

A common conceptual error: treating MSA as a one-time qualification. MSA is repeated whenever a gage is replaced, an operator is trained, the procedure changes, or as part of periodic calibration. The validated gage R&R is part of the PPAP and gets re-validated through the product lifecycle.

## SPC — control charts and capability

SPC (Statistical Process Control) monitors the production process against control limits derived from process data itself. The most common chart is the **X̄–R chart** (average-and-range chart) for variable data; **p-chart** and **c-chart** for attribute data.

Control limits (UCL, LCL) are calculated from process data, typically as the mean ± 3σ. A point outside the control limits, or a run of points trending toward a limit, signals the process has drifted — investigate before producing scrap. SPC is real-time; it's what the operator monitors during a shift.

**Process capability indices** are calculated from the SPC data:

- **Cpk** — short-term capability. The process spread vs the specification spread, accounting for centering. Cpk ≥ 1.33 is the common threshold for "capable" in automotive.
- **Ppk** — long-term performance. Same math as Cpk but using long-term sigma rather than within-subgroup sigma. Ppk ≥ 1.67 is common for new production launch.

The relationship: Cpk is what the process is capable of in steady state; Ppk is what the process actually produces over time including drift and special-cause variation. A new launch with Ppk = 1.67 is "production-ready"; a process with Cpk = 1.33 but Ppk = 0.9 has substantial special-cause variation that needs root-causing.

## PPAP — the deliverable

PPAP (Production Part Approval Process) is the package the supplier submits to the customer at the end of APQP Phase 4. The current AIAG PPAP manual (4th edition, 2006, with addendums) defines 18 elements. The customer specifies a **submission level** (1 through 5) that determines which elements are submitted:

- **Level 1** — Part Submission Warrant (PSW) only. For very low-risk parts or repeats.
- **Level 2** — PSW + dimensional and material results, plus a small set of additional elements.
- **Level 3** — PSW + most elements; the default level for new parts.
- **Level 4** — Customer-defined; whatever the customer asks for.
- **Level 5** — PSW + all elements + everything reviewed at the supplier's facility.

The 18 PPAP elements include:

1. Design records
2. Engineering change documents
3. Customer engineering approval
4. Design FMEA
5. Process flow diagrams
6. Process FMEA
7. Control plan
8. Measurement system analysis studies
9. Dimensional results
10. Material, performance test results
11. Initial process studies (SPC capability)
12. Qualified laboratory documentation
13. Appearance approval report (if applicable)
14. Sample production parts
15. Master sample
16. Checking aids
17. Records of compliance to customer-specific requirements
18. Part Submission Warrant (PSW)

Element 18, the PSW, is the cover sheet. Everything else is what the PSW asserts the supplier has produced.

## Producing the verify artifact

The /verify artifact for this lesson is a complete PPAP package for a part of your choosing. The package should:

- Include all 18 elements at Level 3 (the default for new-part submission).
- Be internally consistent — the DFMEA failure modes that flow to the PFMEA should appear in both; the PFMEA failure modes that drive controls should appear in the Control Plan; the MSA studies should reference the gages used in the Dimensional Results.
- Be assembled into a single document (PDF or markdown bundle) with cross-references between elements.
- Compute SHA-256 of the finished package.
- Submit to /verify with the package title "PPAP — [part name]" and receive a signed certificate.
- Append the certificate to the package as Appendix V.

The grading rubric (the same one an OEM applies to a Level 3 PPAP submission):

| Element               | Acceptable                                                          | Incomplete                                    |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| DFMEA                 | Includes design failure modes with S, O, D ranked and AP/RPN        | Generic failure modes copied from template    |
| PFMEA                 | Process failure modes traceable to DFMEA failure modes              | PFMEA disconnected from DFMEA                 |
| Control Plan          | Every PFMEA failure mode that isn't designed-out has a control      | Controls listed without PFMEA traceability    |
| MSA                   | Gage R&R study with documented operators, parts, gage, and result   | "MSA performed" with no numbers               |
| Initial process study | SPC with Ppk computed and ≥ 1.67 (or a documented improvement plan) | Cpk only, or capability index without context |
| PSW                   | Properly signed cover sheet                                         | Missing or unsigned                           |
| Hash anchor           | /verify certificate present in Appendix V                           | Missing or invalid                            |
