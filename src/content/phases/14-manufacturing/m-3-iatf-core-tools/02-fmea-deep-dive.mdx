## Why this matters

In this illustrative scenario, a company spent three months developing a new engine bracket. The PPAP was rejected on the first submission. The finding: the DFMEA identified "incorrect material grade causes bracket fatigue fracture (S=9, safety)" as the highest-risk failure mode. The PFMEA — which should have traced back to that effect — listed "material grade variation" as a failure mode with a generic detection control ("incoming inspection"). The PFMEA had no prevention control, the detection control wasn't validated, and there was no link back to the S=9 DFMEA effect.

The OEM's quality engineer summarized the finding in four words: "the FMEAs don't talk."

FMEA done well is the analysis that keeps engineers from building things that hurt people. FMEA done as a template-fill exercise is documentation that reassures no one.

A fire alarm and fire-resistant wiring address different parts of the hazard.

## The mental model

> FMEA distinguishes cause, failure and effect; action priority requires the applicable full rating table and evidence for all three ratings.

## The two FMEAs and where they live in APQP

FMEA is applied twice, at different APQP phases, with different scope:

| FMEA type | APQP phase                               | Question answered                                                      | Primary audience                              |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **DFMEA** | Phase 2 — Product Design and Development | How could the DESIGN fail to perform its intended function?            | Design engineering; customer; field service   |
| **PFMEA** | Phase 3 — Process Design and Development | How could the MANUFACTURING PROCESS fail to produce a conforming part? | Manufacturing engineering; quality; operators |

The **DFMEA** analyzes the design: what failure modes could the product exhibit, what are the effects on the user or system, what causes exist at the design level, and what design controls (reviews, testing, analysis) detect or prevent each failure mode.

The **PFMEA** analyzes the manufacturing process: what process steps could go wrong, what parts of the DESIGN do those process failures affect, what process causes exist, and what process controls (inspection, poka-yoke, SPC, statistical sampling) detect or prevent each process failure.

The **linking requirement**: PFMEA failure effects should trace to DFMEA failure modes. If the DFMEA identifies "incorrect material grade → bracket fatigue fracture (S=9)," the PFMEA must have a failure mode addressing how the process could introduce or allow the wrong material grade — with a corresponding severity that reflects the DFMEA's S=9 consequence. A PFMEA failure mode with S=9 consequence must not have only detection controls with no prevention.

## The AIAG-VDA 2019 harmonized framework

Prior to 2019, North American automotive used the AIAG FMEA manual (4th edition, 2008) exclusively. European OEMs (VDA — Verband der Automobilindustrie) used the VDA FMEAs approach. The 2019 AIAG-VDA harmonized handbook merged them. The major structural change: **Action Priority (AP)** became the primary risk-ranking output, with RPN retained as informational.

### Why AP differs from RPN

RPN multiplies severity, occurrence and detection ratings. Different consequences can produce similar products, so sorting solely by RPN hides information. Action Priority uses the applicable full table and all three ratings. **Severity 9 or 10 does not automatically imply High for every occurrence/detection combination.** Do not use a condensed severity-only substitute.

[AIAG explains](https://www.aiag.org/docs/default-source/quality-/new_aiag_vda_fmea_-whitepaper.pdf?sfvrsn=6923e9d_4) that the harmonized method replaces RPN with action priorities based on severity, occurrence and detection. Obtain the applicable handbook and customer requirements for actual classifications. Priority guides action; it is not a declaration that a product is safe or an acceptance threshold. A Low classification does not cancel applicable requirements.

For illustrative ratings 10/2/3 and 3/7/8, RPN is 60 and 168 respectively. The higher product does not justify ignoring the more severe effect. Record the rating rationale, prevention and detection evidence, table/version used, action owner and verification after action. Do not claim an AP letter before consulting that table.

## Prevention vs detection controls

FMEA controls fall into two categories:

**Prevention controls** (PC): prevent the failure cause from occurring or reduce its occurrence. Examples: material certification with chemical analysis, mistake-proofing fixtures that reject wrong alloy, process parameters locked in the SPC system.

**Detection controls** (DC): detect the failure cause or failure mode AFTER it has occurred, before it reaches the customer. Examples: incoming inspection, dimensional inspection, functional test.

Detection can reveal a failure after its cause occurs; prevention aims to reduce or eliminate the cause. Distinguish the two explicitly and verify that each claimed control actually works. Reducing a detection rating requires evidence of the detection method's effectiveness, not merely adding an inspection step.

## A traceable worksheet entry

For a bracket, distinguish the function (carry the specified load), failure mode (fracture), effect (loss of the supported function) and possible cause (insufficient section under the defined load spectrum). Specify the actual design requirements and evidence before assigning ratings. A drawing review may prevent an incorrect requirement from being released; a validated analysis and physical test investigate whether the design meets it. Manufacturing controls then address causes such as wrong material or machining below the required section.

Do not treat an illustrative safety factor or proof-load multiplier as a design requirement. Those values require the applicable engineering analysis, specifications and approval. Record actions, owners and due dates, then reassess using evidence after implementation.

## The DFMEA-to-PFMEA traceability link

The PFMEA must identify how the manufacturing process could introduce or allow the DFMEA's failure causes. For the brake bracket example:

**DFMEA item**: Insufficient boss wall thickness (severity justified from the effect; AP determined using the applicable table).
**PFMEA tracing entry**: CNC machining removes excess material, leaving boss wall < minimum dimension.

| Process step     | Failure Mode       | Failure Effect                         | S   | Failure Cause                                | O   | PC                                                           | DC                                              | D   | AP    |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --- | -------------------------------------------- | --- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
| CNC bore boss ID | Boss wall undercut | Same as DFMEA — loss of braking (S=10) | 10  | Tool wear + incorrect offset causes over-cut | 3   | SPC on tool-wear metric; tool-life protocol limits to N cuts | Post-machining CMM inspection of wall thickness | 2   | **H** |

Where the end effect is unchanged, keep its severity consistent across the linked analysis. Identify special characteristics using applicable design and customer criteria; AP alone is not a substitute for that classification. Flow necessary process controls and reaction plans into the control plan, and review the consistency of the evidence before submission.
