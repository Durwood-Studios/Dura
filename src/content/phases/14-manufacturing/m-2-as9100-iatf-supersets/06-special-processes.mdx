## Why this matters

A heat-treated gear passes all dimensional and hardness spot-check inspections. Six months in service, it fails catastrophically. Metallurgical analysis reveals that 30% of the batch was quenched at a temperature 40°F below the minimum specified temperature due to a malfunctioning thermocouple. The under-temperature quench produced inadequate hardness depth; the surface appeared correct; the core was not.

This is why special processes exist as a distinct category. The product evidence — hardness spot check, dimensional conformance — was insufficient to detect the process parameter deviation. Only the thermocouple calibration records and time-temperature charts would have revealed the issue. But the time-temperature charts were not being retained. The special-process control system failed.

## What makes a process "special"

A process is **special** when the resulting product quality cannot be fully verified by subsequent inspection — meaning no practical end-item inspection method can confirm with adequate confidence that the process was performed correctly.

Examples:

| Process                             | Why inspection alone is insufficient                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Welding**                         | Internal weld structure (fusion, porosity, incomplete penetration) requires destructive or radiographic inspection of every part — not practical for production volumes        |
| **Heat treatment**                  | Core microstructure and residual stress state cannot be measured non-destructively; only time-temperature records confirm the process was executed correctly                   |
| **Electroplating / anodizing**      | Coating adhesion and continuity cannot be fully verified by thickness measurement; process chemistry and temperature must be controlled                                        |
| **Painting and primer application** | Adhesion and coverage are partially verifiable but process parameters (surface prep, temperature, humidity, film thickness during application) determine long-term performance |
| **Non-destructive testing (NDT)**   | NDT results depend on operator qualification, equipment calibration, and technique; a poorly qualified UT operator can produce a "pass" that misses cracks                     |
| **Composite laminate layup**        | Fiber orientation, ply overlap, vacuum integrity — all process parameters that cannot be verified on the finished part                                                         |

## AS9100D §8.5.1.2 — Special process requirements

AS9100D §8.5.1.2 requires the organization to:

1. **Qualify special processes and their equipment and personnel prior to use** — process qualification demonstrates that the process, when operated within specified parameters, produces conforming product. Equipment qualification verifies that the equipment is capable of maintaining required process parameters. Personnel qualification verifies that operators have the knowledge and skills to execute the process.

2. **Define criteria for review and approval of special processes** — documented criteria for what constitutes acceptable process performance. This is the process specification: AMS 2770 (heat treat), AWS D1.1 (structural welding), AMS 2402 (cadmium plating). The criteria define acceptable parameter ranges.

3. **Define the monitoring and measurement requirements, including process control methods, for use during and after execution** — in-process monitoring of critical process parameters (thermocouple readings, chemical bath pH, welding travel speed) and post-process verification (hardness test, radiographic inspection, plating thickness).

4. **Retain documented information of special process results** — time-temperature charts, furnace load records, chemical bath logs, operator signatures. These records are the evidence that the process was performed correctly. For aerospace, retention periods are often life-of-program plus 7 years.

## NADCAP — the aerospace industry special-process accreditation

**NADCAP** (National Aerospace and Defense Contractors Accreditation Program) is the industry-managed special-process accreditation program for aerospace and defense. NADCAP is operated by the Performance Review Institute (PRI) and is required by most major aerospace primes (Boeing, Airbus, Lockheed Martin, etc.) for any supplier performing special processes on aerospace parts.

NADCAP covers these commodity categories:

- Chemical processing (plating, anodizing, passivation)
- Heat treating (solution treatment, age hardening, stress relief)
- Non-destructive testing (UT, RT, MT, PT, ET, VT)
- Materials testing laboratories
- Welding (GTAW, GMAW, SMAW, EBW, LBW, FSW)
- Composites manufacturing
- Coatings (painting, thermal spray)
- Electronics (soldering per J-STD-001)
- Fluid distribution systems

### What a NADCAP audit examines

NADCAP uses PRI-developed checklists (available at eAuditNet) for each commodity. For heat treating:

- Furnace calibration records (AMS 2750 — pyrometry standard for aviation heat treating)
- Thermocouple calibration (type, calibration interval, survey frequency)
- Load documentation (parts, positions, time, temperature profile)
- Quench system (media, agitation, temperature, transfer time from furnace to quench)
- Post-process verification testing

An AS9100D supplier performing heat treat who does not have NADCAP accreditation cannot supply most major aerospace primes for heat-treat applications — the customer QMS clause simply says "special processes must be performed at NADCAP-accredited facilities."

## IATF 16949 §8.5.2 — Automotive special processes

IATF 16949 §8.5.2 (control of special processes) has equivalent requirements to AS9100D but references CQI-family assessments instead of NADCAP:

### Personnel qualification (§8.5.2.1)

IATF 16949 §8.5.2.1 requires that personnel performing special processes are qualified. The qualification must be documented — certification records, training records, skill verifications.

For welding in automotive: AWS D1.1 Welding Procedure Specification (WPS) and Welder Performance Qualification (WPQ) records. A welder must be qualified to the specific process (GTAW, GMAW), position, base material, and filler metal they are using.

### CQI process audits

The Automotive Industry Action Group (AIAG) publishes **CQI (Continuous Quality Improvement)** assessments for common automotive special processes:

- **CQI-9** — Special Process Heat Treat System Assessment. Used for qualification and periodic audit of heat treat operations.
- **CQI-11** — Special Process Plating System Assessment. Covers electroplating, phosphate coating, and chromate conversion.
- **CQI-12** — Special Process Coating System Assessment. Covers painting and primer application.
- **CQI-23** — Special Process Molding System Assessment. Covers injection molding processes.

CQI assessments are not a certification program (unlike NADCAP) — they are self-assessment or customer-audit tools. An IATF 16949 supplier performing heat treat must conduct a CQI-9 assessment of their heat-treat process and address any findings. The customer can use the CQI-9 assessment results during supplier qualification.

## The process parameter monitoring requirement in practice

The most common special-process audit finding: **process parameters documented on paper but not actually monitored in real time.** Examples:

- Heat treat furnace has a thermocouple and chart recorder; charts are filed; the charts show temperature excursions that were never investigated because no one was reviewing the charts during production.
- Plating bath has a documented pH specification of 4.2–4.8; pH is measured once per shift; records show pH of 4.9 on two consecutive readings with no documented investigation.

The §8.5.1.2 monitoring requirement is not satisfied by recording parameters — it requires that the records are reviewed and out-of-specification parameters trigger corrective action before more product is processed.
