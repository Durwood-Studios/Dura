## Why this matters

In commercial or industrial manufacturing, a drawing revision change means "update the procedure and make the new part." In aerospace, a drawing revision change can trigger an FAI (re-verification that the production line still makes the new configuration), a configuration change record (which serial numbers were built to which revision), and a customer notification (if the change affects form, fit, or function). The stakes are different because the consequences of sending the wrong configuration to an aircraft are not a warranty claim — they are an airworthiness risk.

The structural lesson: aerospace quality is not "make it to the drawing." It is "make it to the drawing, know which drawing, prove which serial number was built to which drawing, maintain that record for the life of the program, and have a formal change process for any deviation from that baseline." Configuration management is the system; FAI is the proof; traceability is the audit trail.

## Configuration management — the four activities

AS9100D §8.1.2 requires the organization to establish a configuration management process with four components:

### 1. Configuration identification

Every product, sub-assembly, component, and process document that is part of the deliverable must be identified with a configuration label. Typically this means:

- **Part numbers** with revision letters (or change-notice numbers) for every part.
- **Drawing numbers** with revision status.
- **Document identifiers** for specifications, test procedures, and inspection plans — each with revision control.
- **Serial numbers** (or lot numbers for undifferentiated quantities) that tie specific physical units to specific configuration states.

The **configuration baseline** is the documented set of requirements and design documentation that defines a product at a specific point in its lifecycle. An aircraft program might have a "production baseline" for each design change, with serial numbers mapped to which baseline they were built to.

### 2. Configuration control

Once a baseline is established, no change is permitted without passing through the change-control process. The typical mechanism is a **Change Control Board (CCB)** — a cross-functional team that reviews proposed changes for impact, approves or rejects them, and authorizes implementation.

AS9100D §8.5.6 extends ISO 9001's §8.5.6 change control with aerospace-specific rigor: changes that affect airworthiness require customer or regulatory authority (FAA, EASA) notification. A supplier who makes a process change that affects form, fit, or function of a flight-critical part without notifying the customer is in violation of AS9100D and potentially of the Federal Aviation Regulations.

### 3. Status accounting

Configuration status accounting is the continuous record of where each configuration item stands: which revision is released for production, which serial numbers were built to which revision, which changes are in progress, and which units were built before a change was released and therefore need retrofit, rework, or a concession.

Status accounting answers: "which drawing revision was unit serial number 042 built to?" Without status accounting, this question cannot be answered from records — and an aerospace quality audit routinely asks exactly this question.

### 4. Configuration audit

Two types of audit verify that the physical product matches the documented configuration:

- **Functional Configuration Audit (FCA):** verifies that the item has achieved the performance required by its functional or allocated configuration documentation.
- **Physical Configuration Audit (PCA):** verifies that the as-built configuration of the item matches its product configuration documentation — the physical item is inspected against the drawing, and any discrepancies are documented.

## AS9102 First Article Inspection

**AS9102** (Aerospace First Article Inspection, current revision: AS9102B) is the standard that defines how to conduct a First Article Inspection for aerospace parts. A FAI is a complete, documented verification that the production process can produce a part that meets all requirements on the engineering drawing — before production quantities are authorized.

### When is an FAI required?

AS9100D and customer contracts typically require a new FAI for:

- New part numbers entering production
- Significant design changes (engineering change that affects form, fit, or function)
- Supplier changes (the part is now sourced from a different supplier)
- Significant manufacturing process changes (new machine, new cutting tool family, new heat treatment provider)
- After a lapse in production (typically >2 years without building the part)

A **partial re-FAI** (sometimes called an incremental FAI) covers only the characteristics affected by the change, not all characteristics. The threshold for full vs. partial re-FAI is defined by the customer's requirements.

### The three AS9102 forms

FAI is not divided into a design-characteristics Part 1 and a manufacturing-characteristics Part 2. The reporting structure uses three forms:

- **Form 1: Part Number Accountability** identifies the part, drawing/configuration and report scope.
- **Form 2: Product Accountability** accounts for materials, special processes and functional testing.
- **Form 3: Characteristic Accountability, Verification and Compatibility Evaluation** records the applicable characteristic requirements and results.

Use the [IAQG 9102 resources](https://access.iaqg.org/standard/9102-first-article-inspection-requirement/) and the revision required by the customer for the actual forms and completion instructions. A ballooned drawing links characteristic identifiers to results; it does not replace part identity or material/process evidence. Resolve discrepancies through the applicable process and clearly distinguish complete from incomplete FAI status.

### The FAI package

The complete FAI package contains:

- Ballooned drawing with characteristic list
- Dimensional inspection report (actual values for the applicable Form 3 characteristics)
- Material test reports (material certifications traceable to the heat of material used)
- Special process certifications
- First article part or reference photos
- Statement of conformance signed by Quality Manager

The FAI package is retained for the life of the program and must be available for customer or regulatory review.

## Traceability chain

The traceability chain AS9100D requires connecting:

```
Customer Order → Work Order → Production Router → Drawing Number + Revision
                                                        ↓
              Serial Number of Delivered Unit ← Build Record (what was done)
                                                        ↓
                                               FAI Package (process capability verified)
                                                        ↓
                                               Material Certifications (raw material traceability)
```

Every link in this chain must be documented. An auditor can pick up a serial number, trace backward through the build record to the drawing revision, verify the FAI was conducted for that revision, and verify the material used is traceable to a certified heat of material.
