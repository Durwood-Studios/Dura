## Why this matters

Two boards can look alike while being ordered for different service requirements. Inspecting against an assumed class can accept the wrong workmanship or reject acceptable product. The first useful inspection decision is identifying the requirements that actually apply.

## Start with something familiar

A raincoat and a diving suit both keep water away, but their acceptance tests depend on the intended service. An inspector needs the specified requirement before judging a seam. Electronic assembly inspection has the same need for a declared service category and agreed criteria.

## The mental model

> A product class sets a workmanship requirement context; a particular criterion and revision determine the decision. A class label alone does not prove reliability or safety.

## Three service categories

IPC-A-610 addresses post-assembly acceptance. A printed circuit board assembly (PCBA) is a board with mounted electronic components. Class 1 concerns general electronic products; Class 2 concerns dedicated service; Class 3 concerns high-performance or harsh-environment products. These broad descriptions support communication, but an industry name does not automatically assign every product its class. Confirm the customer or design authority's specified class and any applicable addenda.

IPC J-STD-001 addresses soldering materials and process requirements; it serves a different purpose from inspecting the finished assembly against A-610. Passing a visual criterion does not prove that every required process control was followed. Neither document replaces a product's functional or safety qualification.

## Make a decision from the applicable criterion

Record the document revision, class, characteristic, inspection method, observation, and criterion used. A defect is a failure to meet an applicable acceptance requirement. A process indicator, where specified, is not automatically a product defect; its process response comes from the applicable requirements and the organization's control plan. Do not invent one universal rework rule for all indicators.

Older training material may use categories, illustrations, or thresholds that differ from the contracted revision. A remembered target image is not a substitute for the current applicable criterion. This page intentionally supplies no universal solder-fill percentage: joint type, revision, class, and contract can change what must be checked.

## An inspection record that another person can review

For a training board, use an explicitly supplied fictional criterion: requirement Q-17 says exposed conductor is unacceptable for this assembly. Observation A shows an exposed conductor; observation B shows intact insulation. A fails Q-17 and B meets that one criterion. Neither observation proves the entire board conforms. If the inspector cannot establish whether the conductor is exposed, the correct result is unresolved pending a suitable inspection method, not an invented pass.

## Guided practice

A purchase order specifies Class 2. A technician changes the inspection sheet to Class 3 because the customer is an aerospace company. Explain the missing decision.

<details>
<summary>Reveal the reasoning checkpoint</summary>

The technician has changed the requirement without authority. Confirm the contractual class and any addenda with the responsible authority. Customer industry alone does not authorize replacing the specified inspection basis.

</details>

<summary>Work through a second case</summary>

Explain why a Class 3 label and a clean-looking board do not prove the board meets every required acceptance criterion. Use the fictional Q-17 requirement and distinguish an observed defect from an unresolved observation.

The label identifies the required context, not completed evidence. Each applicable characteristic needs inspection under the relevant revision and method. Exposed conductor fails the supplied Q-17 criterion. An unclear image leaves Q-17 unresolved and requires better evidence; it cannot be counted as a pass. Other criteria and functional tests remain separate.

</details>

## What you learned

- Distinguish acceptance evidence from process control and product qualification.
- Identify when an observation is unresolved rather than demonstrated conforming.

## Source and scope

[IPC's revision-J announcement](https://www.ipc.org/news-release/ipc-releases-j-revisions-two-leading-standards-electronics-assembly) distinguishes A-610 acceptance from J-STD-001 process requirements. Numerical acceptance limits must come from the applicable licensed standard and contract; Q-17 is a teaching example.
