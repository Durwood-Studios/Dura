## Why this matters

Making a board power on again does not establish that it meets its released requirements. A corrected assembly needs a controlled method and verification record so another inspector can determine what changed and why it can be released.

## Start with something familiar

Replacing a damaged zipper with the specified zipper restores the original design. Sewing on a different closure may restore usefulness while changing the design. Both actions need a clear decision, but they are not the same kind of restoration.

## The mental model

> Disposition selects an authorized action; the procedure controls the work; verification determines whether the resulting assembly can be released.

## Keep three questions separate

Rework aims to bring a nonconforming item back into the specified requirements. Repair makes it acceptable for intended use through an authorized disposition, potentially with a departure from the original requirements. Modification deliberately changes the product configuration. The applicable quality system and customer agreement control authorization and terminology; a technician cannot convert a departure into conformance by renaming it.

IPC-7711/7721 provides procedures concerning electronic assembly rework, modification, and repair. It does not mean any pictured procedure is appropriate for every board. Board construction, component limits, materials, access, operator qualification, and contract restrictions affect the selected method.

## From observation to release

Start with item identity, revision, lot or serial number, and the actual failed requirement. Record the proposed disposition and the person or role authorized to approve it. Select the applicable procedure and document the process parameters and limits it requires. After execution, inspect the affected feature and any consequential damage; perform the specified functional or electrical verification as appropriate. Retain the results and release decision.

Do not invent a universal soldering temperature, heating time, or maximum number of repair attempts. Heat history and material limits matter, and repeating an unsuccessful operation can cause hidden damage. If the approved method's limits are reached, stop and obtain disposition rather than trying until the board works.

## Worked disposition

A connector pin is bent and the released specification requires an undamaged connector. Replacing the connector using an approved method and demonstrating all affected requirements can be rework. Installing a jumper that bypasses a damaged conductor may be a repair or modification requiring different authorization. Electrical continuity alone does not establish mechanical integrity, insulation, or the absence of heat damage.

A good record can show an unsuccessful attempt as well as a successful one. It preserves the board's history instead of recording only the final passing test.

## Guided practice

A technician repairs a lifted land with a jumper, tests continuity, and marks the board 'reworked to original drawing.' What evidence is missing?
