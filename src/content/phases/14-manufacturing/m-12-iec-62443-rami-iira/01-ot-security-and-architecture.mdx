## Why this matters

A connected production cell may need remote support and production reporting while still protecting control availability and product integrity. An architecture review must show which communications are intended, who controls them, and what evidence supports the security claim.

## Start with something familiar

A building plan can show rooms, permitted doorways, and who holds keys. A business plan explains why people need to enter. Both are useful, but neither a room label nor a key purchase proves that the building operates securely.

## The mental model

> Zones group assets with shared security requirements; conduits govern communication between them. Architecture views explain the same system from different concerns, while verification establishes whether requirements are met.

## Requirements, capability, and achieved behavior

ISA/IEC 62443 addresses industrial automation and control system security across owners, integrators, and product suppliers. Risk assessment informs zone and conduit requirements. A target security level describes required protection; capability describes what a component or system can support under stated conditions; achieved protection needs evidence from the integrated deployment. A purchased component's label does not automatically certify the surrounding cell.

The levels distinguish increasing resistance to deliberate threats, from casual violations through attackers with greater means, resources, and industrial-system skill. Choosing a target is a documented risk decision, not a rule that every factory gets one particular number or that a sector is forbidden from another. A security-level claim can involve multiple foundational requirement dimensions; reducing it to one marketing number loses context.

For a remote support conduit, define the permitted identities, destination, purpose, approval window, authentication, logging, and shutdown behavior. Those are example design questions, not a claim that a particular level mandates a named product, staffing arrangement, or identical control list in every plant. Functional safety and cybersecurity interact but are not interchangeable assessments.

## Complementary architectural views

RAMI 4.0 organizes industrial concerns across hierarchy, lifecycle, and architecture layers. For example, distinguish a type-level machine design from one installed instance, and distinguish physical equipment from its communicated data and business use. IIRA describes business, usage, functional, and implementation viewpoints. The same data-collection project can be described with both. Customer requirements determine which description is useful; national location does not compel a particular model.

For a cell reporting production totals, the business concern may be inventory accuracy; usage identifies an operator reviewing exceptions; function includes collecting and reconciling counts; implementation names the gateway and approved data path. None of those descriptions proves the gateway's access control works.

## Review the boundary

A diagram showing CELL, GATEWAY, and BUSINESS zones is incomplete until it states allowed communication and failure behavior. A business reporting connection does not imply a permitted business-to-controller command path. Record the actual requirement and test results instead of claiming a generic architecture diagram establishes IEC 62443 conformance.
