## Why this matters

A planning system can promise the same bearing to two orders if it repeatedly reads unchanged available stock. It can also count a quarantined lot as usable material. Reliable planning connects quantity calculations with time, allocation, and quality status.

## Start with something familiar

Planning two dinner parties from the same pantry requires combining the guest counts or updating stock after the first reservation. Counting the same carton of eggs twice produces a plan that cannot be cooked. Material planning has the same conservation requirement.

## The mental model

> Net demand against genuinely available supply, then create dependent requirements for what must still be made. Preserve timing, allocation, and quality status so one unit of stock is not promised twice.

## Gross demand, net demand, and component demand

A bill of materials (BOM) states which components and quantities make a parent assembly. Material requirements planning (MRP) uses demand, inventory, scheduled receipts, and planning rules to propose supply. Enterprise resource planning (ERP) connects that plan to other business records. A planned order is a recommendation; it is not automatically an authorized purchase or completed production.

In a single-period teaching model, two orders need 3 and 4 assemblies. There are 2 usable completed assemblies. Combined gross demand is 7 and the remaining build requirement is 5. Each assembly requires 4 bearings, so dependent bearing demand is 20. Exploding all7 before accounting for completed parents would overstate this example's new bearing requirement.

There are 6 usable bearings and a policy reserve of 2. Available supply for this demand is 4, so the proposed bearing receipt is 16. Do not independently subtract the same4 bearings from each order. A real time-phased plan must respect dates, allocations, lot-size rules, yields, and existing receipts; this single-period arithmetic does not implement all MRP behavior.

## Timing follows dependencies

Use integer calendar-day numbers for a second teaching assumption: finished assemblies are due day 20 and need 2 days to build. Components must be ready by assembly start day 18. If bearings require 5 days of procurement lead time, release is day 13 for receipt day 18. Scheduling every component backward from day 20 would make these bearings arrive after assembly should start. Real calendars and operation overlaps may change the calculation.

## Quality status changes availability

Usable stock excludes material held under the applicable quality procedure. A certificate of conformance is a supplier declaration, not a replacement for required receiving evidence. First-article, production approval, sampling, and retention requirements depend on the sector, contract, and procedure; there is no universal rule that every industry uses the same first-article form or ten-year record period.

Link part and revision, supplier, purchase-order line, lot or serial identity, received quantity, inspection evidence, and disposition. If a receiving observation is unresolved, preserve that status and route it for the authorized decision. Missing or empty inspection data is not a pass. A release into usable inventory must follow the defined authority and evidence, not merely the presence of a document number.

A software implementation also validates BOM cycles, quantities, duplicate records, and identifiers. A recursive walk with no cycle handling is not a production planning engine.

Net parent build is 5, requiring 20 bearings. Initially 4 bearings are available after the reserve, so planned receipt is 16. If 2 of the 6 are held, usable stock becomes 4 and availability after reserve becomes 2, increasing planned receipt to 18. Do not count held material or reuse stock across orders.
