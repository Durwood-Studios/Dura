## Why this matters

A service has three replicas, so a team assumes one can fail without affecting users. That is a hypothesis. A controlled experiment turns it into bounded evidence only if its scope, measurements, abort conditions and recovery are defined first.

## Start with something familiar

A fire drill tests a plan with a declared start, observers and a way to stop. Randomly blocking an exit is not a better drill. Failure injection similarly needs operational control rather than surprise damage.

## The mental model

> An experiment tests a specific resilience hypothesis under stated conditions. Define steady state, blast radius, abort signals and recovery before injecting a failure; successful recovery does not prove immunity to all failures.

## The mechanism

Use an isolated staging service with three replicas and a synthetic workload. Hypothesis: removing one replica under100 requests/second keeps successful responses at or above99% and p95 below200ms. These are supplied teaching acceptance criteria, not universal production thresholds.

Baseline: three replicas,100 requests/second,0.1% errors,p95 90ms. Injection: remove one selected replica for at most60 seconds. Observed: two replicas,100 requests/second,0.5% errors,p95 140ms. Recovery: three replicas,0.1% errors,p95 95ms. This trace is consistent with the hypothesis for the tested workload and failure, but says nothing about losing two replicas, a shared database, different traffic or a regional outage.

Before the run, assign an experiment owner and a separate observer, verify no production identifiers or credentials are targeted, and prove the stop/recovery procedure in the isolated environment. Define abort if error rate exceeds1% over the declared observation interval, p95 exceeds200ms, monitoring becomes unavailable or impact crosses the authorized boundary. A missing monitor is not a passing signal.

Record the exact target, configuration, workload, timestamps, injected fault and restoration evidence. Continue observing after restoration: healthy replica count alone does not prove queues drained or user-visible behavior recovered. An experiment that produces an unexpected result is useful evidence, not a reason to hide the failure or redefine thresholds afterward.

This lesson uses a supplied trace and a written experiment design. It does not send traffic or terminate a process. Execute a real experiment only in the environment and authority specified by its reviewed plan. Broader production testing requires an independently justified risk decision and recovery capability; a classroom plan is not that approval.

## Practice

If monitoring stops during the 60-second injection while replica count still looks correct, should the experiment continue until the timer expires?
