## Why this matters

A forecast ends the day with positive cash, but a payment fails at noon before an expected receipt arrives. Treasury operations must consider when usable funds exist, not only the final total.

## Start with something familiar

A bus ticket available tomorrow cannot pay for today’s journey. Future receipts and current usable liquidity likewise are not interchangeable.

## The mental model

> Project available funds by currency, account and time using confirmed constraints and conservative receipt assumptions. A solvent-looking final balance does not prove every intraday obligation can settle.

## The mechanism

The fictional account starts with 100 units of usable cash. A payment of 120 is due at 12:00 and a receipt of 80 is expected at 15:00. If no approved funding is available, the noon obligation has a 20-unit shortfall even though the projected closing balance is 60. The expected receipt is not yet settled cash.

```python
opening = 100
noon_due = 120
afternoon_expected = 80
shortfall = max(0, noon_due - opening)
closing_projection = opening - noon_due + afternoon_expected
assert shortfall == 20
assert closing_projection == 60
assert opening + afternoon_expected - noon_due >= 0
print("intraday liquidity checks passed")
```

The final assertion deliberately shows why a daily net check misses the noon problem. Record event timestamps, currency, legal account entity, available balance, reservations, settlement cutoffs and confidence/status of receipts. A restricted balance or cash in another currency/account is not automatically usable for this obligation.

Possible responses require the organization’s authority and actual service contracts: escalate the shortfall, obtain approved funding, or renegotiate/reschedule a payment where permitted. The lesson does not recommend borrowing, investing or moving real funds. Never invent an overdraft, assume an FX conversion can settle in time or count the same collateral twice.

Stress the plan by delaying the expected receipt to tomorrow and increasing the due payment to 130. The immediate shortfall becomes 30; the projected cash after the payment and before any delayed receipt is -30, which is a funding gap rather than permission to overdraft. Record who decides, what evidence authorizes the action and how actual bank statements reconcile with forecasts.

Operational controls include separate preparation/approval where required, immutable payment identities, verified beneficiary changes and exception monitoring. Reconcile actual settled flows back to the forecast and explain differences. This synthetic schedule is not a treasury management system or evidence that a regulated liquidity requirement is satisfied.
