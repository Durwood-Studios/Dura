## Why this matters

A dataset has an access policy, but nobody owns its definition, retention or correction process. Governance turns a collection of tables into an accountable information product with clear responsibilities and evidence.

## Start with something familiar

A borrowed laboratory instrument needs an owner, intended use, calibration record and return rules. A dataset similarly needs more than a label saying confidential; people must know what it means and how to handle changes or errors.

## The mental model

> Governance assigns accountable decisions about meaning, quality, access, lineage and lifecycle. A policy is useful only when its controls and exception process can be observed and reviewed.

## The mechanism

Consider a fictional support analytics dataset. Raw records include ticket_id, customer_email, category and resolution_minutes. The intended purpose is a weekly category-level service report. Direct customer email is unnecessary for that aggregate report and should not be copied merely because it is available upstream.

A dataset contract names an accountable owner, a steward for routine quality work, approved purposes, field definitions, access roles, provenance and a retention decision. For this exercise only, the organization’s approved schedule says raw identified extracts expire after 30 days and aggregate reports after 365 days. These are supplied organizational choices, not universal legal retention periods. Real retention must reflect applicable obligations and justified business needs.

Lineage records source system/version, transformation revision and output identity so a correction can be traced. If resolution_minutes changes from elapsed calendar time to working time, silently retaining the same metric name makes old and new reports incomparable. Version the definition, communicate the change and decide whether to recompute history or show a documented break.

Quality controls include uniqueness, allowed categories, missing-value rates and reconciliation to source totals. An alert needs an owner and response policy; counting bad rows without deciding whether to publish them is incomplete. Access reviews should verify actual grants, not only a diagram. Deletion tests need to cover copies, exports and backups according to the organization’s documented lifecycle and restoration procedures.

A privacy framework helps organize risk management but does not itself establish legal compliance. Minimize fields and access according to the use, document exceptions and review them. Avoid putting real personal data in a training fixture. Governance evidence should show the decision, its implementation and the review outcome rather than a claim that having a policy file makes a dataset safe.
