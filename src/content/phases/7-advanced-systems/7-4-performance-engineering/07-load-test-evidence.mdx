## Why this matters

A service looks faster under heavy load because it rejects most requests immediately. Reporting only a latency percentile can make failure look like improvement. A load test must preserve the relationship between offered work, completed work and failed work.

## Start with something familiar

A busy restaurant can shorten its average service time by turning customers away at the door. That does not mean it served dinner faster for everyone who arrived. Demand, accepted work and rejected work belong in the same report.

## The mental model

> Latency is conditional on the observations included in it. Record offered rate, completion rate, errors and backlog alongside percentiles, and ensure the load generator itself can sustain the intended workload.

## The mechanism

A closed workload waits for a response before a virtual user sends another request; as responses slow, offered rate can fall. An open arrival schedule attempts the declared rate independently of response time, subject to generator limits. Neither is universally correct: choose the model that resembles the question you are testing, and report achieved rather than merely configured load.

The synthetic report below measures each steady interval for60 seconds. Successful-request p95 excludes failed requests, so the error column is essential. There is no claim that these values were measured from a deployed service.

| Offered requests/second | Completed requests/second | Error rate | Successful p95 | Backlog at end |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|100|100|0.1%|90ms|0|
|200|200|0.5%|150ms|0|
|300|250|20%|80ms|3000|

At300 offered and250 completed per second,50 requests/second accumulate: over60 seconds,3000 requests remain outstanding, assuming the stated counts describe arrivals and all completions consistently. The lower successful p95 does not indicate a healthy improvement; the system rejects or fails substantial work and accumulates backlog.

For this fixture, acceptance requires error rate below1%, successful p95 below200ms and no growing backlog. The200-rate interval meets the observed criteria; the 300 interval does not. Do not infer that201 or any untested rate is sustainable. Repeat with realistic payloads, warmup, duration and dependency behavior, and inspect generator CPU/network limits and scheduling misses.

A production decision also needs uncertainty and headroom. A single run is not a capacity guarantee. Preserve request counts and the inclusion policy for timeouts, cancellations and retries. Otherwise the percentile may exclude exactly the slowest user experiences. Stop a controlled test at predefined safety and resource bounds rather than exhausting an unrelated shared environment.
