## Why this matters

A product promises free learning, then its growth plan proposes charging for the same lessons. Revenue design must account for commitments and user trust as well as arithmetic.

## Start with something familiar

A public library can rent an event room without charging patrons to read the books it promised to provide freely. The separate service has its own costs and value.

## The mental model

> Define what is being sold, who pays, the billing unit and the unchanged product commitment. Compare contribution and user consequences under explicit assumptions; revenue does not justify deceptive or contradictory access rules.

## The mechanism

Common patterns include one-time purchase, recurring subscription, usage-based billing, voluntary donations and paid services around a free product. Each needs a clear value proposition, predictable terms and appropriate cancellation/refund handling. These are business-model categories, not instructions to add payment gates to Dura.

Dura’s core curriculum and existing learning tools remain free. A fictional separate managed-hosting service can illustrate unit economics without restricting them. Suppose its monthly price is 40 units, variable hosting/support cost is 25 per customer and fixed monthly service overhead is 300. Contribution is 15 per customer; 20 customers cover that stated fixed overhead, before acquisition, taxes, financing and other omitted costs.

```python
price = 40
variable_cost = 25
fixed_overhead = 300
contribution = price - variable_cost
assert contribution == 15
assert 20 * contribution - fixed_overhead == 0
assert 10 * contribution - fixed_overhead == -150
print("service contribution checks passed")
```

If support cost rises by 10 per customer, contribution falls to 5 and 60 customers cover the same overhead. This sensitivity is more informative than labeling the model profitable from its price alone. It is a synthetic planning calculation, not a pricing recommendation or financial forecast.

For usage billing, define a meter, included quantity, limits and dispute/reconciliation process so users can predict charges. For a subscription, define what renews and how to stop renewal. For donations, state that giving unlocks nothing. Avoid dark patterns, undisclosed charges or tying essential promised access to unrelated purchases.

Before proposing a revenue experiment, record the hypothesis, customer problem, cost assumptions, success/stop criteria and effects on existing users. Keep the core promise as an invariant in product and tests. A separate product may be evaluated on its own merits; this lesson does not implement billing or alter Dura’s access model.
