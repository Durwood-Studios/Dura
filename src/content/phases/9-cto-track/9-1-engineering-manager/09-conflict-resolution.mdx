## Why this matters

Two engineers disagree about a database migration and stop reviewing each other’s work. A manager who merely chooses the louder proposal may end the meeting while leaving the technical risk and working relationship unresolved.

## Start with something familiar

Two people sharing a workshop may disagree about moving a heavy machine. Asking what each needs protected can reveal a common objective even when their proposed locations differ. A useful decision records constraints, not a verdict about who is difficult.

## The mental model

> Separate observable facts, interpretations, interests and decision authority. Resolve the technical choice with evidence and the working agreement with explicit commitments; do not infer motives from disagreement.

## The mechanism

Begin with specific behavior: two reviews waited three days, and the release decision lacks an owner. Ask each person to explain their concern and check that you can restate it accurately. One wants a delivery date; the other wants a tested rollback. These interests may be compatible through a staged migration.

Use a shared decision record: objective, constraints, alternatives, evidence, owner, deadline and revisit condition. For this fictional case, option A is a one-hour cutover with an untested rollback. Option B is a two-day staged migration with one extra engineer-day of work and a tested revert. The customer permits a three-day window, and irreversible data loss is unacceptable. On these facts B has a defensible advantage; if staffing or reversibility evidence changes, revisit the comparison.

Do not promise that every conflict is a misunderstanding. Bullying, discrimination or threats require the organization’s appropriate reporting and support processes, not pressure to negotiate privately. Mediation uses an impartial facilitator and voluntary participation; a line manager making a delivery decision should not mislabel that role as neutral mediation.

Agree how work continues: who reviews which artifact, when concerns are raised, and how unresolved risks escalate. Check the agreement after the decision rather than assuming that a meeting repaired trust. Keep sensitive personal details out of a broadly shared technical record. Record the operational decision and necessary responsibilities, not speculative character judgments.

This exercise teaches a bounded engineering disagreement. It is not legal advice or a complete workplace-investigation process. The source describes mediation principles; the migration scenario and decision rubric are Dura-authored practice.

## Practice

One engineer says the other “does not care about quality.” Rewrite that as an observable concern that can be investigated without assigning a motive.
