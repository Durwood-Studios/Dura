## Why this matters

A model describes an image confidently, but the image was cropped and the text prompt supplied the missing detail. A useful multimodal test must distinguish what the inputs support from what the system merely associates with them.

## Start with something familiar

A translator matching a photograph to a caption must check that both refer to the same scene. A fluent caption is not evidence that the pictured object exists, and a missing photograph cannot be replaced silently with a plausible guess.

## The mental model

> Modalities have separate encoders, preprocessing and missing-data risks. Matching representations can support retrieval; a similarity score is not automatically a calibrated probability or a verified factual description.

## The mechanism

A modality is a form of input such as text, image or audio. In a dual-encoder design, an image encoder and a text encoder map inputs into comparable representations. Contrastive training encourages matched pairs to score higher than mismatched pairs. Other multimodal architectures combine modalities through cross-attention or feed image representations into a language model; not every system has the same structure.

Consider this synthetic image-to-caption score table. Scores are illustrative, not measurements from CLIP or any deployed model.

| Image | Caption A: red valve closed | Caption B: blue valve open |
|---|---:|---:|
| I1, known red closed valve |0.80|0.20|
| I2, known blue open valve |0.60|0.55|

Choosing the largest score retrieves A for both images: one of two labels is correct. For I2, the margin is only 0.05, but a small margin threshold must be selected on a separate development set, not invented after viewing this final example. Even a large margin can be wrong outside the development distribution.

Record the original input identity, transformation such as resize or crop, candidate captions, model version and scoring rule. A crop that removes valve position changes what the image can establish. A text-only fallback may still be useful, but it must not claim to have inspected unavailable pixels. Treat instructions found within an image or caption as untrusted content when connecting the model to tools.

Test mismatched pairs, missing images, unreadable detail, contradictory text and image, and relevant subgroups or lighting conditions. Compare image-only, text-only and combined behavior where the task permits it: a combined system may exploit a shortcut instead of integrating evidence. The goal is a task-specific evidence contract, not a claim that one benchmark establishes universal visual understanding.
